Additional short Results quotations: "FA-treated sample"; "enhance its DNA affinity".

Purified intact HxlR: 100 uM protein, 1 mM formaldehyde, 20 mM HEPES pH 7.5,
300 mM NaCl, 1 h at 37 C, desalted before FT-MS. Supplementary Figure 1c
shows new +12-Da protein peaks after treatment, alongside remaining unmodified
and sodium-adduct species. This is an observed chemical-product response.
The C11/K13 methylene assignment uses additional crystallographic density,
MS/MS and synthetic peptide experiments, not the intact mass shift alone.
Cellular MS/MS uses BL21(DE3) HxlR expressors exposed to 0.6 mM formaldehyde
for 1 h before harvest, followed by HisTrap purification and tryptic digest.
The F9-K23 peptide localizes the mass change. Synthetic Ac-FNCEKEL-NH2,
treated with 1 mM formaldehyde for 1 h at 37 C, gains +12 and +30 species.
C11A loses both; K13A loses +12 but retains +30. These isolated N-terminal
peptides lie outside the current Pfam interval. The sequential reaction
scheme is a proposal, not a directly time-resolved trajectory.

EMSA Methods: pretreat 100 uM HxlR +/- 1 mM formaldehyde, 1-2 h, 37 C,
20 mM HEPES pH 7.5, 300 mM NaCl. Dilute into 20 ul containing 50 nM
annealed DNA, 20 mM HEPES pH 7.0, 50 mM KCl, 5 mM MgCl2, 10% glycerol.
The Figure 1e gel and captions instead label 0.6 mM formaldehyde; this
1-versus-0.6-mM difference is unresolved, not reconciled by invented dilution.
Figure 1e compares WT and C11A at 0.2/0.4/0.6 uM protein: WT shifts more
DNA after treatment; C11A has little treatment response but retains binding.
Supplementary Figure 2d uses a different K13A range, 0.05/0.1/0.15/0.2 uM,
and shows binding with little change on treatment. Do not mistake K13A for
a binding-null mutant or compare absolute WT/K13A affinities from unmatched
concentrations. No fitted KD is adopted. Captions report three independent
experiments with representative gels.
Table 2 gives BRH-F CCTCACAGTATCCTCCAAGTAACTTGTTGACTTCAAAGTGCCTACTTCTC
and its reverse complement for EMSA; this 50-bp probe is not the optimized
palindromic 20-bp crystallization DNA.

Cellular reporter: E. coli BW25113, pBAD-HxlR and a B. subtilis 168
hxlAB-promoter GFP reporter, overnight diluted 1:100 in LB to OD600 0.6;
4 mM arabinose for 1 h, then +/- 600 uM formaldehyde for 40 min.
Flow cytometry excitation 488 nm, emission 530/30BP. Figure 1f shows a
WT fluorescence shift and little C11A response; C11A also has lower basal
signal. Supplementary Figure 2e shows higher K13A basal signal and little
additional formaldehyde response. Supplementary 2b/c retain colony-count
viability checks and gating, not proof of equal mutant abundance or folding.
This reporter endpoint is not a direct transcription-rate or detoxification
measurement and is not a native B. subtilis cellular experiment.

Purified pET28a protein has an N-terminal His tag removed with thrombin,
followed by DTT treatment and SEC; PDB 7BZD retains GSH before native sequence.
The pBAD reporter is a different construct. Table 2 pBAD-HxlR-F includes
CCATGGGCCGGATG, compatible with MGRM rather than reference MSRM if translated
from that ATG; Methods instead describe Gibson assembly. Thus a primer-implied
S2G possibility, not a verified plasmid mutation, remains unresolved. Figure
labels WT/C11A/K13A are retained as the authors' construct labels.
Static structures differ in both formaldehyde and DNA conditions; a full
helix-flipping causal trajectory is not adopted as directly observed. FAsor
fusions, methylglyoxal responses, reversibility, other DUF24 members and
the 2005 native-operon study require separate scoped research.
