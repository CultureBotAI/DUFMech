IDENTITY AND DOMAIN BOUNDARY
Frozen PF01977.23 is UbiD, former DUF117/UPF0096, IPR048304, EX_DUF.
It is the central RIFT-related domain, not the entire enzyme or the D partner.
The October 10 worklist SHA256 is
17fb625876c9326ed284263a47bcd84e0d7844a31c040dc06cd2260c36f1c383.

ShC: UniProt Q9S4M7 sequence 1, entry 75, 480 aa, shdC/ohb1,
Sedimentibacter hydroxybenzoicus (formerly Clostridium hydroxybenzoicum),
NCBITaxon:29345. InterPro's complete three-result response maps PF01977
106-312, PF20695 12-89 and PF20696 319-442 (one-based inclusive).
ShD: Q4R102 sequence 1, entry 28, 68 aa, shdD, same taxon.
Its complete InterPro response maps PF26358 2-42, not PF01977.
7AE4 (closed), 7AE5 (open) and 7AE7 (V59X) entity 1 deposited bodies
are identical to Q9S4M7. Entities 2 of 7AE4/7AE5 equal Q4R102;
7AE7 entity 2 equals Q4R102 residues 1-58. The deleted 59-68 tail is
VIPPIPPLKK. Deposited sequences are not a claim that assay proteins lack tags.
ShC L182 is inside PF01977; T325/S326 hinge and L425 are outside it.
ShD C3/C6/C28/C31 coordinate Zn in the apo structure, not an assay proving
isolated zinc binding sufficient for decarboxylation. No isolated PF01977
fragment was tested for activity; domain-level sufficiency remains unproven.

BsC reference candidate: P94405 sequence 1, entry 148, 473 aa, bsdC/ubiD/yclC,
B. subtilis strain 168, NCBITaxon:224308. PF01977 106-308, PF20695 10-88,
PF20696 314-438. BsD reference candidate C0H3U9 sequence 1, entry 74,
75 aa, bsdD, same reference strain; PF26358 1-51. C0SPB5 (yclD) is a
different 154-aa entry and is NOT substituted for the small BsdD partner.
The paper identifies synthetic B. subtilis genes but does not give an exact
strain/accession/complete synthetic insert. Supplement Table 2 BsC N-primer
translates to MAYQDFREF, matching P94405. The reverse-primer homology segment
TTCATCAGATCCATCAGTTTTTGTTC has reverse complement
GAACAAAAACTGATGGATCTGATGAA; its complete in-frame codons encode EQKLMDLM.
That matches an internal C-terminal segment, not the complete P94405 tail
EQKLMDLMNK. The partial terminal codon and vector junction prevent an exact
full-construct identity assertion. BsD terminal primer encodes VPERKA, matching
C0H3U9's terminus. Biopython 1.88 was used for these translations.
The archived docking models concern ShVdcCD; inspection of all 325 ZIP member
names and Information.txt did not supply a Bs synthetic insert or model
sequence. Graphs describe the published Bs constructs at species scope, using
these accessions only as reference mappings, not exact experimental sequences.
This uncertainty is not evidence that the central domain is absent.

V1. ENDPOINT SUBSTRATE AND PARTNER ASSAYS: Figure 2a, source sheet Fig 2a
Methods: E. coli BL21(DE3) expression; C-terminal His C or N-terminal His D
and untagged partners in the listed pET vectors. Coexpressed complexes were
purified by affinity/size exclusion; isolated C and D were also mixed in vitro.
These are distinct preparations, not interchangeable labels. Preferred ShCD
purification used N-His D. UbiX coexpression did not yield holo VdcCD; in vitro
prFMN reconstitution was required. UbiX/Fre were filtered out of cofactor
preparations before addition to decarboxylation assays.
HPLC reactions: 10 uM C, 5 mM substrate, protein:prFMN:MnCl2 1:2:2,
18 h in a nitrogen anoxic chamber. Initial PHB/vanillate stock: 10 mM in
100 mM potassium phosphate pH 6, diluted for assay; substrates were not
oxygen-purged. Cognate mixed partners: 10 uM each. Noncognate mixtures used
20 uM D with 10 uM C. Acetonitrile/TFA quench; C18 HPLC; authentic substrate
and expected-product standards and concentration curves. Substrate plus
20 uM prFMN reconstitution mix was the no-protein side-reaction control.
Two independent replicates. These are endpoint yields, not kinetic constants.

Substrate order: PHB; vanillic; protocatechuic; 3-Me-4-hydroxybenzoic;
gallic; syringic acid. Corresponding decarboxylated products are phenol,
guaiacol, catechol, 2-methylphenol, pyrogallol and 2,6-dimethoxyphenol.
The graph labels use the substrate-assigned HPLC products; direct CO2 release
was not measured by this assay. Percent mean +/- SEM shown in Figure 2a:
BsC alone: 0; 5.8 +/- 0; remaining substrates not determined.
BsD alone: 0; 0; remaining not determined.
BsC + BsD: 98.9 +/- 1.1; 100; remaining not determined.
ShC alone: 0; 0.7 +/- 0.7; remaining not determined.
ShD alone: 0; 0; remaining not determined.
ShC + ShD: 100; 97.9 +/- 1.1; remaining not determined.
Co-purified BsCD: 99.2 +/- 0.8; 99.6 +/- 0.3; 99.6 +/- 0.1;
97.8 +/- 1.9; 1.1 +/- 1.1; 99.8 +/- 0.1.
BsC + ShD: 0.9 +/- 0.1; 29 +/- 1.8; 0; 8.9 +/- 0.2; 0; 0.
Co-purified ShCD: 100; 100; 99.9 +/- 0.1; 99.7 +/- 0.1; 5.3 +/- 0.5; 0.
ShC + BsD: 9.3 +/- 0.3; 1.7 +/- 0; 2.7 +/- 0.3; 3.8 +/- 0.5; 0; 0.
ShCD V59X: 100; 91.5 +/- 3.7; 99.6 +/- 0.3; 99.9 +/- 0.1; 0; 0.
The source workbook resolves rounded zeros and low endpoints: BsC vanillate
replicates are 5.83864/5.74359%; ShC 1.38854/0%; BsCD gallic 0/2.16910%;
ShCD gallic 4.78839/5.79144%; V59X gallic 0/0%. Blank cells mean not
determined, not zero. The text's strict partner requirement does not erase
low C-alone vanillate activity. The text's Sh-only gallic claim is qualified
by a nonzero BsCD replicate in the table. Detection limits are not reported.
Noncognate products show limited activity; mismatched D ratios and preparation
differences do not identify a matched, isolated causal effect of partner identity.
Minimal quotations: "considerable activity in contrast to isolated VdcC";
"limited activation"; "substrate scope".

V2. PARTNER STOICHIOMETRY: Figure 2b and source sheet Fig 2b
Anaerobic reconstitution: ShC 50 uM, ShD 10 or 200 uM, prFMN/MnCl2
200 uM each. Assay: 0.25 uM C, 100 uM PHB, A247 decrease over 3 h;
substrate not oxygen-purged. At 90 min the 4:1 D:C rates are
18.02083/17.86111/16.53472 uM/min versus 7.45139/7.03472/8.18750 at
0.2:1. Three technical replicates; trend in three independent repeats.
Higher D availability increases measured activity at fixed C in this context;
it does not prove the stoichiometry of each active catalytic assembly or rule
out differences in assembly, maturation or active-cofactor occupancy.
Quotation: "slower decarboxylation of PHB".

V3. OXYGEN EXPOSURE AND PARTIAL COFACTOR RESCUE: Figure 2c, source Fig 2c
Anaerobic ShCD reconstitution with two equivalents prFMN/Mn, then desalting.
Protein was kept in an opaque open tube exposed to air for 90 min; aliquots
assayed with 100 uM PHB in 50 mM NaPi/50 mM KCl pH 6 by A247.
Mean plotted activity: 2 min 2.49 s^-1 (2.61/2.37); 90 min 0.855
(0.94/0.77). At 90 min sample returned to anoxic chamber, two equivalents
prFMN added, then immediately reassayed: 2.09035/1.86647, mean 1.97841 s^-1.
This is partial recovery (~79.5% of 2-min mean), without added Mn. Two
independent replicates; reported approximate activity half-life 40 min was not
refitted here. Cofactor-related inactivation is consistent with rescue, but no
chemical lesion or oxidation product was directly measured. Time in air is
the perturbation, not a controlled oxygen concentration series. Do not
transfer these observations to AnFdc's separate light-inactivation assay.
Quotation: "partially restores activity".

V4. VISCOSOGENS: Figure 4d and source sheet Fig 4d
PHB 50-750 uM in 50 mM NaPi/50 mM KCl pH 6 with no viscosogen, 45%
v/v glycerol or 40% w/v sucrose. A247 rates; pathlength 1 cm at <=100 uM
and 0.2 cm at >=250 uM; 100 uL assay. Buffer n=3, each viscosogen n=2
technical replicates; similar trends in two independent repeats.
Source fit kcat_app (s^-1): buffer 0.856 [0.7607,0.9686], sucrose 0.341
[0.2518,0.4757], glycerol 0.244 [0.1885,0.3407], brackets 95% limits.
Figure labels Km_app: buffer 127 [89,182] uM; sucrose 85 [23,241];
glycerol 183 [88,416]. Intervals overlap; no significant Km change claimed.
Relative viscosity was estimated from literature, not measured here. Figure
axis says eta (cP), whereas prose describes relative viscosity; do not turn
these estimates into a new experimentally measured physical parameter.
prFMN:CD:Mn 0.5:1:2, apparent active concentration assumed equal to prFMN
assuming complete UbiX conversion. Stock Et_app 30 uM; 1:3 aerobic:anaerobic
buffer, overnight trace-oxygen maturation. Actual active occupancy not measured.
The viscosity-associated rate decrease supports a dynamic model but does not
uniquely identify domain motion as its cause or exclude other solvent effects.
Quotation: "drastically reduced".

V5. VARIANTS: Figure 2a, source Fig 2a, specificity text, supplement tables
ShD V59X removes residues 59-68; 7AE7 confirms the 58-aa deposited partner.
Most endpoint yields remain near WT; vanillate falls from 100 to 91.5% mean,
while the small gallic-acid signal falls from 5.3% to undetected. Do not
translate <10 percentage-point yield change into uniform relative retention.
No syringate product was detected in either WT or V59X. The tail is not
universally essential for activity, but this does not show every D function
dispensable. The deletion acts in PF26358 partner, not PF01977.
The source text reports ShC L182V syringic acid turnover 6.5 +/- 0.6%
versus undetected WT. This exact result is not in Figure 2a's table or any
of the five source workbook sheets. Report it as a text-only experimental
result, not inspected raw replicates or an independently reproduced assay.
L182 maps within PF01977; improved acceptance need not uniquely demonstrate
steric-volume causality versus folding, assembly or active-cofactor effects.
Supplement Table 2 labels both L182V primers Q5F, an unresolved label issue.
Minimal quotations: "yields only slightly diminished"; "6.5".

V6. STRUCTURE AND PROPOSED DYNAMIC CHEMISTRY: Figures 3-5, supplements 2-5
Structures are apo ShCD; no holo ShCD or isolated subunit crystal obtained.
Closed 7AE4, open 7AE5, V59X 7AE7 resolve 3.31, 2.19 and 2.66 A,
respectively. The C hexamer has six D partners. Both open and closed forms
contain D but lack prFMN: these are not measured ligand-triggered transitions.
DynDom identifies ~32-degree hinge rotation at T325/S326, <1 A translation.
Modeling used closed residues 1-323 plus open oligomerization domain to make
a hybrid, then 16 Chimera morph models. DFT intermediates were optimized
with Gaussian09 B3LYP/6-311++G(d,p), D3/BJ dispersion and dielectric 5.7.
AutoDock Vina docked them to these models; prFMN geometry was fixed after
Rosetta docking; side-chain conformers fixed except chosen E278 rotamer for
PHB to avoid clashes. Selected substrate poses require the anticipated
carboxy/hydroxy orientation. Normal-mode similarity is qualitative support,
not observed catalytic dynamics. Docking energies are not activation barriers.
Int2 prefers more open models; Int3Quinoid a more closed model (~10 A R-L).
The proposed route uses closure to strain aromatic Int2 and assist quinoid
conversion and product release. No VdcCD intermediate trajectory/adduct
trapping or causal hinge perturbation establishes this sequence experimentally.
No observed edge is inferred merely from the existence of open/closed structures.
D contacts near ERE loops suggest allosteric stabilization, not direct proof
of loop stabilization as the activity-restoring route. Cofactor/partner/PHB
must be present jointly in catalytic hypotheses; D alone is not sufficient.
Quotations: "domain closure leads to a strained conformation";
"maintaining the structure of the key catalytic ERE motif".

NUMBERING AND CONSTRUCT LIMITS
Q9S4M7 and all three deposited C bodies have R168, G178, E273, E278,
L182, T325, S326 and L425. Prose/supplement Figure 4 use R168, main
Figures 3-5 show R178, supplement Figure 3 image shows R138 while its caption
uses R168. Preserve these inconsistent labels; do not curate G178 as Arg or
quietly alter the source. R-L distances are source-reported model metrics,
not newly calculated structural measurements here. Bs insert/vector junction
and L182V raw replicates need additional primary clarification.
The AnFdc graphs and their distinct source discrepancies remain unchanged.
Unreviewed ScFdc, EcUbiD, AroY, HmfF, HudA and later specificity/maturation
work remain part of the family-wide objective, not an assertion of no evidence.
