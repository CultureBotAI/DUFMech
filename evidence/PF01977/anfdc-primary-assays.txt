S1a, Figure1c-d / UV-Vis methods: purified AnFdc coexpressed with EcUbiX
consumes cinnamic acid and forms the styrene-assigned UV peak. Assay: 350 uL,
50 mM KCl, 50 mM sodium phosphate pH6, 25 C, 1-mm path. The 270-nm signal
falls as the 245-nm signal rises. This is a complete-protein reaction with
bound prFMN, not a domain-only test or a direct CO2 measurement.
S1b, Extended Data1 / carboxylation methods: 10 mM styrene, 100 mM sodium
phosphate initially pH6 and 3 M bicarbonate (final pH7.5), 25 C overnight.
The enzyme-containing sample has the cinnamic-acid-assigned HPLC peak absent
from the no-enzyme control. Figure ordinate says A254, methods say detection
264 nm. Retain this discrepancy, not an invented corrected wavelength. The
enzyme amount and raw integration/calibration series are not established here.
S1c, Extended Data8 / GC methods: separate 10 mM 2-hexenoic, 2-heptenoic or
2-octenoic acid assays, 500 uL, 50 mM KCl/50 mM sodium phosphate pH6, 25 C,
shaking, enzyme versus no-enzyme, headspace GC-FID with authentic standards.
Products are respectively 1-pentene, 1-hexene and 1-heptene. Exact enzyme
amount/incubation duration are not supplied by this method. The caption reports
no detected 1-octene from 2-nonenoic acid; that negative has no separate panel.
The third displayed panel is red enzyme/black control, conflicting with the
caption's color description. Product labels, standards and paired control,
not the erroneous color prose, identify the retained outcome.
S1d: phenylpyruvate inhibition/recovery, NaBH3CN inactivation and other substrate
kinetics are identified but not yet represented as separate graphs. The 2015
atomistic scheme is a proposal, not a directly observed sequence of all steps.

S2a: AnFdc pET30a, EcUbiX pET21b, E. coli BL21(DE3); intended mutations
sequence-confirmed. MnCl2 supplied at induction; purified complete proteins.
Rate assays follow cinnamic acid consumption in 350 uL 50 mM KCl/50 mM
sodium phosphate pH6 at 25 C. Apparent kcat uses total protein, not an exact
active-prFMN occupancy correction. HPLC: 10 mM cinnamic acid, 50 uM enzyme,
same salts/pH, 2 h at 25 C; standards for styrene and cinnamic acid are shown.
S2b, Figures5/6: E282D retains activity (apparent kcat 0.63 +/-0.04 s^-1),
E277D 1.2 +/-0.2 s^-1 versus WT 9.4 +/-0.1 s^-1. E277D does not follow
Michaelis-Menten kinetics; no fitted Km is adopted. E277Q has small product
formation (reported upper kcat bound 0.3 min^-1); R173A also has low residual
product formation. E282Q and R173K have no detected turnover in these assays.
This refines the 2015 blanket inactivity statement for R173A and E277Q.
Loss/reduction does not by itself assign a proton-transfer step. R173A has
cofactor-maturation defects; R173K was not crystallized; preparation-dependent
occupancy/adduct differences and structural perturbations remain relevant.
S2c, Figure7 / NMR methods: 35 uM protein, styrene-saturated D2O with 50 mM
potassium phosphate pH6, 298 K, no-enzyme control. Loss of the 5.3-ppm proton
and simplification of the 6.9-ppm signal establish trans-C1 exchange. WT and
E282D exchange within the approximately 20-minute dead time; E277D reaches
approximately 90% after 2 h. Results report very low R173A exchange at 4 h;
the inspected Figure7 does not display that separate four-hour spectrum.
E277Q, E282Q and R173K lack detectable/significant exchange under the tested
conditions. Exchange is not measured net carboxylation or isotope-traced CO2.
S2d, Figure2: ambient-light exposure on ice reduces WT, E277D and E282D
activity compared with dark controls. The WT assay concentration is 45 nM;
E277D 300 nM and E282D 600 nM. This is an observed perturbation effect,
not a directly time-resolved molecular causal trajectory. Five minutes of
365-nm UV exposure of dark-purified WT is reported to abolish activity.
Separately, dark-grown WT crystals contain iminium prFMN; illuminated crystals
show at least approximately 50% ketimine, without reversion after returning to
darkness. E282Q crystals also form ketimine after UV exposure. Acid/base
details of that photoisomerization remain proposed, not demonstrated edges.

S3a: E282Q exposed to cinnamic acid traps decarboxylated Int2 (6R3F), not a
complete turnover cycle. Supplement p4 specifies 1 mM cinnamic acid during
expression from 2 h after induction and during purification. WT rapidly soaked
crystals show mixed Int3/product/inactive-cofactor occupancies, not isolated
intermediates followed in one active site through time. Glu282 proton transfer
and a computed Int1-open pathway are not directly observed transitions.
S3b: WT phenylpropiolic acid rapid soaks trap Int1-prime; prolonged
cocrystallization traps Int3-prime. S4 shows the corresponding cofactor mass
shift from about 669 to 625 over 0/21/50 h in solution. This is slow adduct
decarboxylation, not demonstrated free phenylacetylene turnover. The L439G
control retains the higher-mass species through 48 h. Crystals: 4 C, 1 mM
ligand for cocrystallization, 100 mM for soaks. Exact solution time-course
temperature and concentration are not established by the cited MS methods.
S3c: S5 shows reduced but nonzero cinnamic-acid turnover for F437L and L439G.
F437L apparent kcat 0.39 +/-0.01 s^-1, Km 5.3 +/-1.0 uM; do not report
zero cinnamic-acid turnover from the separate alkyne-adduct negative result.
These residues are in PF20696, not PF01977. Rates remain apparent.
S3d: butynoic/crotonic adduct trapping, phenylacetylene addition, substrate
exchange, and DFT strain proposals remain extension targets. Binding, trapped
structures, time-course MS and computed transition states are distinct evidence
types. No observed graph chains together different mutants or alkyne/alkene
substrates as if they were one measured catalytic trajectory.
