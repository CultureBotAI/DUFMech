Reference taxon 559292 does not establish the experimental yeast strain.
Species-level graph taxon is 4932; E. coli is the expression host.

Lin2015 Methods S10 identifies codon-optimized synthetic YDR539W/YDR538W.
Singly expressed FDC is pET28b-derived, N-terminal six-His tagged; the
coexpression preparation has C-terminal six-His FDC and untagged tPAD1

WT-parent comparison is not independently established by these methods.

L1: Cinnamate conversion and pH dependence, S2/S12, Figures 2 and 3
Routine GC-MS: 500 uL PBS pH 8, 6.7 mM cinnamic acid, typically 1 uM
FDC, 32 C, variable incubation, ethyl-acetate extraction. Styrene is
quantified against authentic styrene calibration standards. N-His FDC
purified from BL21 has reported activity about 0.54 umol/min/mg. This
supports whole-preparation chemistry, not isolated-domain sufficiency,
native yeast flux, directly measured CO2 or a complete atomic pathway.
For pH measurements incubation is 10 min; substrate saturation was checked.
Figure 2C shows much lower styrene at pH 6/6.5 than 7/7.5 within the
same phosphate-buffer regime (50 mM potassium phosphate, 100 mM NaCl).
At pH 8 the phosphate and Tris series differ; pH 8-9 uses Tris/Cl.
No single catalytic pKa or purely pH-driven cross-buffer comparison is inferred.
Figure 2B peak label is 57899.1529 Da versus prose 57898.15 +/-0.5 Da;
the stated predicted mass is 57897.8 Da. Do not silently reconcile these.
Native PAGE's approximately 480-kDa migration is not a definitive oligomer
stoichiometry and is not equated with the later native-MS dimer assignment.

L2: Dialysis and lysate rescue, S2 and original Figure 3
24-h dialysis through a 3.5-kDa-cutoff membrane greatly lowers activity.
The plotted dialyzed-FDC bar is small but nonzero (roughly 5% visually).
Fresh WT BL21 lysate raises it above the as-isolated 100% reference
(roughly 240% visually), not merely back to exactly 100%. Predialyzed
lysate yields a much lower, nonzero bar (roughly 15%). These are visual
approximations, not digitized/raw values. Replicate count and error-bar
definition were not established in the inspected caption/methods.
The three directed comparisons are dialysis versus isolated preparation,
fresh-lysate addition to dialyzed FDC, and predialyzed versus fresh lysate
as donors. Complex-lysate constituents, occupancy and stability remain
unresolved; no individual cofactor-synthesis reaction is directly measured.

L3: Host preparation and direct-mix rescue, S3/S4, Figure 4
FDC from the reported delta-ubiX host has very low activity compared with
the WT-derived preparation; the figure shows small baseline bars rather
than an assay-independent proof of zero. Adding delta-ubiX lysate alone
does not visibly increase the baseline. Purified N-His tPAD1 alone raises
activity to roughly 50% of the Figure 3 isolated-WT reference, whereas
tPAD1 plus delta-ubiX lysate gives roughly 800%. The joint condition,
not either component alone, is the strongly activating preparation.
These approximate bar heights are not raw quantitative data. Typical
routine concentrations are FDC 1 uM and tPAD1 10 uM, not separately
confirmed amounts for every plotted bar. WT-lysate activation of the
delta-host preparation is reported in S3 but not separately plotted in
Figure 4. This text-only edge retains that distinction.
tPAD1 alone does not directly decarboxylate cinnamate (S4), distinct from
its ability to activate an FDC-containing assay. Do not attribute styrene
production to PAD1. No exact tPAD1 truncation grounding is asserted.

L4: Membrane-separated activation, S5 and Figure 6
Receiver: 1 uM apo-FDC with 6.7 mM cinnamate; donor and receiver separated
by 3.5-kDa-cutoff membrane. Donors are WT BL21 lysate, delta-ubiX lysate,
10 uM tPAD1, or delta-ubiX lysate plus 10 uM tPAD1. GC-MS time course
to 120 min shows increasing styrene for WT donor, tPAD1 donor and the
joint donor; delta-ubiX donor stays at baseline. The joint donor rises
fastest in the displayed experiment. Exact compartment volumes, protein
leakage controls and diffusible species identity were not established.
Transferable activation is consistent with a small cofactor; tPAD1-alone
activation may reflect copurified material. It is not proof of de novo
cofactor synthesis from no supplied precursor or of a particular contact
mechanism. Direct-mix and membrane-separated assays are separate graphs.

L5: Coexpression/construct-associated activity, S6/S10
C-His FDC from the tandem untagged-tPAD1 construct has reported specific
activity 4.3 +/-0.2 umol styrene/min/mg, versus about 0.54 for singly
expressed N-His FDC. The joint preparation change is observed. No
tag-matched single-factor comparison or measured occupancy correction
is supplied. The 342-nm chromophore and higher activity motivate, but
do not by themselves quantify, cofactor loading.

L6: Apparent kinetics, S7/S13 and Table 1
Separate substrates in 100 mM potassium phosphate pH 7, 250-500 nM
high-activity coexpressed FDC, 0.05-1.5 mM substrate; Results says 25 C,
Methods says room temperature. Readouts: A304 cinnamate, A334 p-coumarate,
A344 ferulate; stated extinction coefficients 1140, 988 and 1330 M^-1cm^-1.
Apparent kcat (s^-1), Km (uM), respectively:
cinnamate 4.6 +/-0.2, 180 +/-24;
ferulate 3.8 +/-0.3, 180 +/-41;
p-coumarate 1.5 +/-0.1, 110 +/-26.
The XML table labels its middle column Kn while the prose and efficiency
column use Km; the abstract's lower kcat bound 1.4 differs from Table 1's
1.5. Values above follow Table 1. These are total-protein-normalized UV
substrate-depletion proxies, not measured active-site turnover constants.
The proposed 20-25% occupancy assumes a reduced-FMN-like extinction
coefficient. Do not publish the proposed 4-5-fold correction as measured.
The new graph records each absorbance decrease, without inventing direct
product-MS assignments for ferulate or coumarate in these assays.

L7: Transferable FDC-derived fraction and stability, S6 (text-only effects)
Protein precipitation with 1:1 dichloromethane/methanol, followed by
solvent removal, produces a fraction reported to activate apo-FDC.
This is functional transfer from a preparation, not proof that one pure
identified molecular species is sufficient. The source's major m/z
661.2212 +/-0.0132 (Figure 8 caption 661.2279) could be a fragment;
the 2015 study does not resolve its structure. Do not equate that ion
with a proven intact prFMN species using a later paper's interpretation.
Pretreatment with 0.1 M HCl for 1 h or 0.1 M NaOH for 1 h, then
neutralization, loses activating activity. Mild 0.1% formic acid retains
activity. Exact dose, quantitative rescue traces, neutralization-matched
controls and precise degradation chemistry are not established here.
FMN detection among base-treated products does not uniquely define a
reaction path. Acid/base pretreatment effects are unresolved, not direct
observations of a specified chemical-bond cleavage.

Open follow-up, not family completion
Retrieve and review Ferguson2016 (PMID:27119435), Ferguson2017
(PMID:28753302) main-paper contexts, native yeast and later ScFdc
maturation/specificity work. The accessible 2017 SI has been inspected
as a lead: a potassium-associated prFMN-FNVB composition and native-MS
shifts do not alone establish unique cycloaddition bond topology or an
entire catalytic trajectory. No new graph adopts that lead in this pass.
Resolve synthetic FDC/tPAD1 inserts, truncation numbering, host matching,
tag-matched preparation controls, mass/table discrepancies, raw low bars,
active occupancy and domain-specific causality. Existing AnFdc/VdcCD
findings and EcUbiD, AroY, HmfF, HudA coverage remain open. A full-text
access failure is a coverage limit, never a negative biological result.
