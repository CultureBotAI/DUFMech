GEL partner abundance:
Figure1c compares whole-cell WT and knockout HEK293 lysates. TMCO1 knockout
strongly reduces C20orf24/OPTI signal, while other complexes remain substantially
detectable. Extended Data1a independently shows reciprocal depletion effects:
TMCO1 siRNA reduces OPTI, and OPTI siRNA reduces TMCO1, versus non-targeting
control; tubulin is the loading control. ED1a was repeated twice; Figure1c was
partially replicated rather than formally repeated as a full experiment.
These are steady-state protein dependencies, not measured degradation rates,
direct binding kinetics or proof of which partner stabilizes the other first.
The exact ED1a depletion duration is not separately specified; do not silently
copy the fluorescent-reporter timing into this whole-cell immunoblot assay.

Cell-free YIPF1 glycosylation:
Figures4d/e use N-terminal HA-YIPF1 translated in RRL with WT or knockout
HEK293-derived rough microsomes, 50 min at 32 C. The glycosylated fraction
decreases with TMCO1 knockout and with joint TMCO1/Nicalin or TMCO1/CCDC47
knockout versus WT. Quantification has three biological replicates, mean/SD,
ordinary one-way ANOVA/Dunnett comparison to WT. No exact P value is invented.
ASGR1 glycosylation and TMED2 signal-peptide processing remain substantially
preserved. ED5a/b shows that higher microsome amounts do not normalize YIPF1.
In the separate Flag-YIPF1 experiment, Figure4b carbonate-resistant recovery is
comparable while glycosylation falls. This is inconsistent with claiming simple
loss of all membrane insertion. Figure4a N297A removes the glycan site and
supports band assignment; it is not a TMCO1 mutation.

Cell-free protection endpoint:
Figure4c uses radiolabeled C-terminal YIPF1-HA, not N-terminal HA-YIPF1.
Default IVT is 45 min at 32 C; isolated membranes are treated with proteinase K
0.5 mg/ml on ice for 45 min, quenched with PMSF and analysed with HA IP.
The recovered protected-fragment signal is about 60% of WT in TMCO1-knockout
microsomes. No-microsome and no-PK conditions support interpretation. Five
repeats are reported. This is a topology-sensitive proxy in a complex membrane
preparation, not a directly measured TMCO1 insertion rate or purified sufficiency.
Loss of TMCO1 also affects OPTI and recruitment of other multipass components.

Cellular glycosylation:
Figures4f/g use transient N-terminal Flag-YIPF1 in WT and single-knockout cells,
not the in vitro double-knockout series. After transfection, 1 ng/ml doxycycline
induces expression for 12 h. TMCO1 knockout lowers glycosylated fraction in five
biological replicates. These data do not measure ion conduction, client transport
activity or the rate of any individual insertion step.

Reporter effects:
Figures4i/j: TMCO1 siRNA lowers the YIPF1, EAAT1 and AGTR2 client/control
fluorescence ratios compared with non-targeting siRNA. YIPF1 is GFP-P2A-RFP-YIPF1
(RFP/GFP), whereas EAAT1 and AGTR2 carry client-GFP-P2A-RFP (GFP/RFP).
ASGR1 is the largely preserved single-pass control. The stable Flp-In T-REx
293 reporter cells receive two siRNA treatments, 48 h then about 24 h, followed
by 1000 ng/ml doxycycline for 6 h. Two repeats of Figures4i/j are reported;
the displayed histograms do not establish an exact turnover constant.
TMCO1 siRNA is s29085; OPTI siRNA is s31821; control is 4390843. ED5e checks
target/partner depletion. No rescue or independent second TMCO1 siRNA is shown
in these selected experiments. The flow Methods describe gating on soluble
reporter levels but then name GFP-positive EAAT1/AGTR2 and RFP-positive
ASGR1/YIPF1 populations, which are the client-linked colors in the constructs.
This wording mismatch is retained, not silently repaired or used to invent
unreported gates. Culture antibiotic values also need stock/final clarification.

All retained perturbation effects have unresolved molecular mediation. The
glycosylation/protection controls support a topology interpretation, but do not
isolate TMCO1 from the rest of GEL or the multipass machinery. No phenotype-to-
phenotype causal chain is inferred across the cell-free and cellular assays.
Earlier intermediates, substrate-driven recruitment variants, accompanying
structural studies, complete clone sequences and later contexts remain open.
