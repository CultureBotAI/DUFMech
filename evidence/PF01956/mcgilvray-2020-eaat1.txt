Minimal quotation, Results: "reduced".
Figure5E-G: endogenous EAAT1 immunoblot signal in HEK293 lysates is lower than
WT in TMCO1 knockout (about threefold), TMCO1/Nicalin double knockout (about
2.8-fold) and TMCO1/CCDC47 double knockout (about 3.8-fold). Figure5G combines
monomer and upper-band signal and reports mean/SD. The upper band is attributed
to SDS-induced aggregation, not a native functional EAAT1 trimer. Do not infer
epistasis or significance of the difference between the knockout genotypes.
Separate Nicalin, CCDC47 and TRAM deletion arms are controls/context, not new
TMCO1-specific perturbations. TRAM deletion does not reproduce the EAAT1 effect.
Tubulin and Ponceau S provide loading comparisons in the displayed experiments.

For each expression replicate, 750,000 cells were plated on poly-L-lysine and
grown overnight; RIPA lysates were immunoblotted and quantified with ImageJ.
Figure5-supplement1B compares EndoH/PNGaseF-sensitive patterns in WT and TMCO1
knockout. Integrin alpha5 and TRAPalpha steady-state abundance and glycosylation
are substantially preserved. This limits a general membrane-protein defect,
not every possible targeting, translation, folding or turnover mechanism.
Figure5-supplement1C GAPDH-normalized EAAT1 RNA changes by less than 1.5-fold;
TMCO1 knockout is visibly lower than WT, about 0.7, not exactly unchanged.
No direct transcription-rate, surface localization or glutamate-flux assay is
this endpoint. No exclusive post-translational mechanism is assigned.

RIP-seq enrichment of multipass transcripts and structural association identify
candidate clients and a plausible translocon, not directed causal effects for
all enriched proteins. The proposed TMCO1/TMEM147 funnels and shielding cavity
are models, not experimentally observed substrate trajectories. The three
retained genotype effects have unresolved molecular mediation. The double
knockouts are joint perturbations, not independent TMCO1 sufficiency.
