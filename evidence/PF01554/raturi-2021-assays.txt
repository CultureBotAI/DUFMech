Curator assessment of Results on ion availability and biochemical binding,
Figures 2A and 4A/B (visually inspected), and Methods on expression,
purification, transport, binding and statistics:
L. lactis NZ9000 lacking lmrA/lmrCD expresses N-terminal His10-tagged NorM-VC.
De-energized cells are washed, loaded with 2 uM ethidium and glucose-energized.
At pH 7 in 50 mM KPi/5 mM MgSO4, 1 mM added Na+ lowers the 400-s fluorescence
endpoint compared with no added Na+; nonexpressing controls do not show the
same sodium response. Measurements use three independent cell preparations.
Methods specify 12.5 mM glucose; Figure 2 caption specifies 20 mM. Preserve
this discrepancy rather than select a concentration. This fluorescence assay
supports enhanced efflux-associated output, not a resolved ionic microstep.

Purified protein in 0.05% DDM is titrated against 2 uM ethidium in 50 mM KPi
pH 7 with 0.2 mM KCl. Final protein spans 0.036-1 uM; 0.5 mM Na2SO4 supplies
1 mM Na+. Anisotropy uses 500/580-nm excitation/emission and about two minutes
equilibration, with matched elution-buffer baseline. Figure 4A shows sodium
enhancement at low WT protein concentrations, not a uniform increase at the
highest concentration. The two-component fit is an interpretation of binding,
not a physical count of binding sites. Li+, Cs+ and Rb+ do not reproduce the
sodium response. D36N, E255Q and D371N each lose the WT sodium enhancement;
residual binding remains, so these are not total binding-null claims.
At 0.2 uM WT protein without added sodium, raising pH 6-9 increases anisotropy
in Figure 4B. Apparent transition midpoints are 6.9 and 8.0; assignment to
specific protonated residues is interpretive. Three independently prepared
protein batches, mean +/- SEM, are reported. Mutant-fold equivalence and the
complete ion-translocation route are not directly proved by these assays.

The Discussion's two pathways and ion stoichiometries combine functional
interpretation with structural/MD models; no atomic trajectory is adopted here.
