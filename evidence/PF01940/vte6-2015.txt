Figure 5B: WT leaf extract with phytyl-P and CTP produces phytyl-PP; extracts
from vte6-1/pst15134 and vte6-2/pst00121 have lower activity. Figure 5D:
VTE5/VTE6-coexpressing Rosetta(DE3) extracts with phytol and CTP outperform
single expressors. These are CRUDE extracts, not purified VTE6. Figure 5C
compares phytol-fed coexpressing cells with VTE5 alone: more phytyl-PP, less
phytyl-P. VTE6 is His-tagged, VTE5 S-tagged; joint input is indispensable.

Figures 2,4,7: insertion mutants have reduced growth, chlorophyll, tocopherol
and phytyl-PP, with increased phytyl-P, free phytol and selected phytyl esters.
Figures 4,6,7: adding vte5-2 partially improves growth/chlorophyll and lowers
phytyl-P; tocopherol deficiency persists. Toxicity remains a hypothesis;
free phytol and other metabolic changes are alternatives. Figure 8: Col-0
35S-VTE6 lines #3/#4 have higher seed phytyl-PP and gamma-tocopherol than
empty-vector controls. The caption calls the comparator WT; actual bars say eV.

Methods: six-week MS/sucrose plants; insertion background not assigned here.
vte6-2 was backcrossed to remove a secondary mutation. Figure 3 measures
genotype recovery among germinated segregating seeds, not survival of a pure
homozygous cohort. Discussion reports unsuccessful complete sll0875 mutant
segregation; this is not direct biochemical proof or conclusive essentiality.
