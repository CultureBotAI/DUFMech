Minimal quotation: "Cas6 is an endoribonuclease".

N-terminal His6 PF1131 from P. furiosus was expressed in E. coli, heat-treated
and Ni-affinity purified. Figure1C compares protein-free and 500 nM Cas6
reactions with repeat-only and repeat-guide-repeat RNA. Both yield cleavage
products, but the guide-containing intermediate is not fully mature crRNA.
Figure3A maps a 22-nt 5' product from the synthetic 30-nt repeat, at A22/A23.
Uniformly transcribed substrates can include additional template-derived bases;
do not assign every band from different labeling preparations a 22-nt length.

Standard reactions: 50 fmol RNA/20 ul (2.5 nM), 20 mM HEPES pH7,
250 mM KCl, 0.75 mM DTT, 1.5 mM MgCl2, 5 ug E. coli tRNA, 10% glycerol,
70 C for 30 min. Binding uses 8% native PAGE; cleavage uses deproteinization
and 12-15% denaturing PAGE. Synthetic-repeat mapping Figure3A uses 5'-terminal
labeling; its specific protein dose is not given in that figure legend.

Figure3C/D: A22/A23-to-GG RNA retains shifted binding but has strongly reduced
cleavage across 1,50,200,500 nM protein. These are paired substrate substitutions,
not independent residue tests. Figure7A shows cleavage without added Mg and in
20 mM EDTA at 1 uM Cas6. This does not exclude every possible metal role.
Figure7B end-labeling supports a 5'-OH and blocked 3' end; the exact cyclic
phosphate assignment is not established by that gel alone.

Text accompanying Figures4/5 reports binding to the 5' cleavage product, not
the 3' product, and no detected binding/cleavage of the tested DNA analogue.
Binding lanes in a cleavage-permissive reaction can therefore contain product,
not exclusively intact substrate. Original Figure4/5 images remain uninspected.
The earlier nt9-12 mutation interpretation is explicitly revised by Wang2011:
those changes also alter the A9/A10 recognition region and cannot establish
an absolute four-nucleotide linker-length requirement. No cellular knockout,
viral challenge, residue-specific chemistry or final crRNA maturation is measured.
