Reconstruction of devT deletion 408-502 and independent deletion 13-541
produced mound formation with poor sporulation; ectopic devT complementation
improved the reconstructed/new mutants but not the old mutant. This supports
a second-site explanation for the old early-development defect, not an exact
identified second-site allele. devI/promoter suppression also works in the new
devT background. The paper revises the 2015 inference of a special DevT role.

Figure 8 is a proposed gene-regulatory model. DevTRS subcomplex assembly,
direct DNA repression and RNA-guided decay are not directly measured there.
The antisense transcript is explicitly unpublished data, and targeting of RNA
or DNA is conditional on its unverified extent. Primary text says the mechanism
of negative autoregulation is unknown. A later review's stronger wording is
not adopted as proof of a demonstrated antisense-guided DevTRS mechanism.
cas6/cas3 null results argue against their requirement for the tested feedback
and sporulation, not against all antiviral or physiological roles.
DevT/DevS protein and exo-expression effects, the six-hour devI timing change
and the devS/exo suppressor tests are not reassigned as DevR-alone effects.
No observed molecular cycle is assembled from this model or from different
archaeal binding experiments. Exact strains/constructs and further primary
DevR contexts still need expanded review.
