Strain-168 yaaD/yaaE were cloned from genomic DNA; constructs were sequenced.
Untagged proteins were expressed in E. coli BL21(DE3), 0.5 mM IPTG, 3 h at
37 C. YaaD purification used DEAE and phenyl-Sepharose; YaaE used DEAE and
hydroxyapatite. N-terminal sequencing and >95% SDS-PAGE purity are reported.
The His6-tagged YaaE preparation is reserved for the pull-out experiment.

Supplement S2A and main Results: 4 uM untagged YaaE with 4 uM YaaD produces
more coupled glutaminase signal than YaaE alone. Assay: 300 ul, 50 mM Tris-Cl
pH 8.0, 0.5 mM APAD, 7 U bovine glutamate dehydrogenase, 10 mM glutamine,
37 C; APAD reduction measured at 363 nm. Individual proteins stay near
background. The readout tracks glutamate through the reporter enzyme, not
directly imaged ammonia transfer or an isolated active-site conformation.
S2B shows partner-ratio dependence; no new universal optimum is inferred.

Product assays: 50 mM Tris-Cl pH 8.0, 37 C, 40 uM each untagged YaaD/YaaE,
10 mM glutamine, 0.5 mM ribose 5-phosphate and 0.5 mM dihydroxyacetone
phosphate. The A414 product readout is a Tris Schiff-base signal. Product
identity is additionally supported by deproteinized HPLC coelution with PLP,
chemical spectral shifts, electrospray MS and S3 600-MHz 1H NMR in D2O.
S3 identifies an unknown contaminant at 3.75 ppm. No raw spectra were reprocessed.
Figure 1C separately tests 20 uM YaaD, 20 uM YaaE when present, 10 mM
ammonium sulfate, 1 mM ribulose or ribose 5-phosphate and 2 mM DL-G3P.
Adding YaaE increases A414 formation with ribulose 5-phosphate, even with
external ammonium. Ribose 5-phosphate works without YaaE; that condition
prevents a universal requirement claim. A proposed isomerase role is not shown.

Figure 3A: 4 uM each YaaD/YaaE preincubated with 5 mM acivicin at 37 C
for displayed 0-10 min intervals, then assayed with glutamine. Longer exposure
reduces coupled glutaminase activity. Figure 3C separately exposes 40 uM each
enzyme to 0.04-5 mM acivicin, 60 min per Methods, and measures A414 synthesis
with 0.5 mM ribose 5-phosphate, 0.5 mM DHAP and 10 mM glutamine. Increasing
inhibitor reduces this signal. These are biochemical, not cellular drug effects.
Figure 6 assigns an acivicin-associated peptide to C79. Figure 7 supports
changed association using tagged pull-out and untagged native-PAGE methods;
neither is adopted as a time-resolved native dissociation cycle in this graph.

Unresolved source discrepancies: Figure 1A/B caption says 1 mM glutamine,
whereas Methods and Results say 10 mM; do not attach exact fitted kinetics to
the chosen 10 mM product graph. Figure 3B caption labels rates s^-1 whereas
Results and the time axis use minutes. Inhibition Methods list 1/2/10 mM,
but Figure 3A/B report 5 mM and 2/5/10 mM. Figure 4 caption says kDa for
values whose main text says Da, and Methods versus Figure 4C differ on 1/2 mM
acivicin. Intact-protein and peptide-adduct mass differences also require raw
data/chemical reconciliation before claiming a precise adduct formula.
The graphs retain observed directions, not resolved kinetic constants, atomic
intermediates, ammonia-channel geometry or general antimicrobial efficacy.
