Inspected material
Results, Discussion and STAR Methods including plasmid construction and
statistics; original Figures 1-5 and Tables 1-2 visually inspected; supplemental
Figures S2/S4/S5 visually inspected and S1-S5 legends read; Tables S1-S3/S5,
S4 plasmid table and S6 synthetic sequences parsed. Raw PRIDE PXD051215 spectra
and Mendeley jrjkkb49vx.1 unprocessed images were not reanalyzed. Main Fig3 and
supplemental S3 are not independent replication of every claimed phenotype.

Native MR-1 phenotypes: Figure 1 and Figure 2B, Table 1
In-frame deletions were PCR/Sanger-checked. WT, delta-maf1, delta-maf2 and the
double mutant were compared. Loss of maf1 strongly reduces spread on 0.25%
LB agar; loss of maf2 increases it. Double loss has very little spread. TEM
shows strongly shortened filaments for maf1 loss and a further shortening in
the double mutant. Complementation restores filament lengths toward WT.
Figure 1D includes 22-32 cells per strain across two experiments; avoid claiming
maf2 deletion leaves length quantitatively identical to WT. Main text describes
similar lengths, whereas the plotted distributions differ. Sheared-fraction
FlaA/B signal decreases for maf1 and double loss; anti-sigma70 checks lysis.
This is not a direct assay of export rate or an isolated glycosylation step.
The Figure 1B legend describes overnight 30 C followed by room temperature;
general Methods describe 2 days at 30 C. Keep this protocol discrepancy visible.

Flagellin glycopeptides: Table 1
FLAG-FlaA and FLAG-FlaB were immunopurified separately. S143-containing peptides
lose detected glycan masses in delta-maf1 but retain modification in delta-maf2.
The S105-containing FlaA peptide loses modification in delta-maf2 and delta-glfM
but retains it in delta-maf1. These are preferences, not exclusive site rules.
FlaB S105 retains one +538 Da match in delta-maf2 and also one in the double
mutant. Authors consider rare promiscuity or carryover; do not claim absolute
absence of all modification in double loss. Other peptides carry multiple
potential serines, and the table's bold positions refer to prior site assignments.
"None", "peptide not observed" and "not analyzed" are distinct outcomes.
Reported filtering: Byonic score >200, mass error 3 ppm. Spectral counts are not
occupancies or biological replicate counts. Native glycan masses include
510/524/538/539 Da and more complex adducts; Pse-derivative assignment combines
MS and pathway dependence, not an independently resolved stereochemical structure.

Acceptor substitutions: Figure 2C; pJU0026-pJU0030
Five simultaneous S-to-A substitutions (105/143/171/180/185), two-site sets
105/180 and 143/185, and three-site sets 105/171/180 and 143/171/185 each change
FLAG-FlaA mobility in delta-flaA cells. The 105/171/180 set resembles delta-maf2.
The 143/171/185 set does not fully resemble delta-maf1. Do not infer that each
single serine is independently necessary, or that the five-site construct
establishes absence of every possible glycan. These are acceptor variants, not
mutations inside PF01973. Reference/native numbering excludes the FLAG tag.

Maf variants: Figures 2D/E and S2
Maf1 D247A fails to restore the WT-like FlaA/B mobility or motility; D333A
partially restores these readouts relative to deletion/empty-vector cells.
Maf2 D261A fails to restore WT-like mobility; D347A partly restores it. Both
Maf2 variants reduce spreading relative to delta-maf2, including under IPTG
induction, despite detectable sheared flagellin. Do not equate reduced spread
with blocked export, or assume the D347A residual modification rescues motility.
Anti-HA panels show expression of these Maf constructs. They do not measure
folding, localization, normalized turnover, acid/base roles or metal coordination.
Maf1 D247/D333 and Maf2 D261/D347 lie in their respective profile intervals.
S2A aligns corresponding regions, but does not establish identical residue
functions across AMB-1 and MR-1. Preserve the AMB-1 evidence separately.
Synthetic Maf2 construct identity has the L230S discrepancy documented in the
mapping ledger. It applies to this expression lineage, not automatically to
genome-amplified BACTH constructs or native deletion experiments.

Maf1 C-terminal deletions and reporter assays: Figure 3, S3 legends
HA-Maf1(1-420), detectable on anti-HA blots, fails to restore modification and
spreading in delta-maf1. HA-Maf1(1-699) yields partial recovery; filaments remain
shorter than full-length complementation in the reported S3E experiment.
Neither truncation is an isolated PF01973 fragment: the profile is 174-338.
TPR prediction 576-822 differs from assayed CTDs 421-822 and 550-822.
In E. coli BTH101, full-length Maf1/FlaA or FlaB T18/T25 combinations give
positive LacZ reporters. Maf1(1-420) combinations have very low signal, whereas
1-699 and isolated CTDs retain orientation-dependent signals. The isolated CTDs
give weaker quantitative signals than full length and are outside PF01973.
Unfused/empty controls, host control and FlgL/GlfM comparison are described.
Five clones were pooled for most quantitative cultures, not five independent
biological replicates. Two CTD421-822 combinations yielded only one clone.
BACTH supports construct-dependent association/proximity, not an affinity,
purified binary contact, catalytic rate or a demonstrated acceptor-positioning
trajectory. Do not convert this assay into an observed binding-to-transfer chain.

GlfM genetics and association: Figure 4, Table 1, Figure S4
GlfM/SO_3260 is a distinct 89-aa partner (PF26154), not a PF01973 member.
delta-glfM reproduces the maf2-like mobility and increased-spread phenotype;
it removes detected S105-containing FlaA glycopeptides. Deleting glfM in
delta-maf1 further increases mobility, similar to double-maf loss.
Expression of GlfM restores the single-deletion pattern toward WT and the
double mutant toward the delta-maf1 parental pattern, not WT glycosylation.
WT GlfM raises the Maf2/FlaA or FlaB BACTH reporter relative to selected
two-component controls. Do not read every differently oriented spot as a
universal negative: the quantitative Figure 4C comparison is the clean test.
FLAG-GlfM pull-down enriches Maf2 (26 unique peptides, 46 spectra, 59% coverage)
versus empty-vector control (3/3/6%). Maf1 also appears weakly (2/2/3%) in
the pull-down. These data do not prove absence of any Maf1 association or exact
ternary stoichiometry. Interaction evidence is context for the causal reporter
and functional effects, not itself an activation edge.
D77A GlfM fails to restore the mobility pattern in both native backgrounds
and fails to promote the selected tripartite BACTH signals. S4C detects
FLAG-GlfM and FLAG-GlfM-D77A: no complete expression-loss claim is warranted.
F24L, R25K and E80A variants retain native complementation under tested conditions;
they are not generally dispensable residues. D77A does not prove a direct
Maf2 contact or catalytic contribution by Asp77. Folding remains untested.

Reconstitution and donor dependence: Figures 5A-C, S1, Table 2
The important glycopeptide comparison uses C. crescentus NA1000-derived
delta-flmG (endogenous flagellin GT removed, Pse pathway present) and delta-neuB
(Pse deficient); the immunoblot also includes WT. Do not relabel all hosts WT.
FLAG-FlaA plus Maf1 yields +359/+375 Da glycopeptides including S143 and the
S105-containing peptide in delta-flmG. Maf2 plus GlfM and FLAG-FlaA yields
+359/+375 Da on the S105-containing peptide. Without GlfM those Maf2-dependent
adducts are not detected. Other Table 2 peptides may be unobserved, so lack of
their entry is not proof that Maf2 can never modify them. Donor-pathway state,
acceptor, enzyme and partner are joint conditions, not independently sufficient.
In delta-neuB, the +359/+375 forms disappear; +220 Da is detected on a
multi-serine FlaA peptide with Maf1 and the S105-containing peptide with
Maf2/GlfM. KDO is a proposed identity for +220, not chemically proven here.
Main-text S181 conflicts with Table 2's S180 and reference Ala181; do not invent
a serine at 181. Combined Maf1/Maf2 with GlfM further retards FLAG-FlaA in
WT Caulobacter, but gel shifts alone do not quantify glycan count per molecule.
Native delta-pseI/delta-pseC controls lose the slow-mobility flagellin form.
These pathway perturbations do not identify the enzyme's atomic transfer step.
The heterologous co-expression system is not purified Maf catalysis and uses
host-generated, non-native glycan derivatives. Its synthetic Maf2 sequence
discrepancy remains unresolved, not silently corrected to reference wild type.

Orthologs and limits: Figure 5D/E, Figure S5, Tables S1/S2/S6
Kurthia Maf/GlfM/Hag has a different outcome across hosts. GlfM promotes
Hag-HA abundance and mobility shift in Shewanella. In Caulobacter, Hag is
detectable without GlfM and there is residual Maf/Pse-dependent shift without
GlfM (explicit S5A legend). GlfM is therefore not shown universally necessary.
Pseudomonas Irchel 3E13 FliC/Maf/GlfM gives a Pse-dependent shift in Caulobacter.
Exact WP_068455574.1 and WP_095156947.1 records were retrieved, but their
COG2604 hits are not verified PF01973 coordinates; these two Maf systems are
retained as mapped-accession leads pending profile/construct verification.
S. putrefaciens CN-32 GlfM WP_011919637.1 restores MR-1 flagellin mobility in
delta-glfM and supports the MR-1 Maf2/FlaB system in Caulobacter. Other tested
GlfM partners (Kurthia, Irchel 3E13, S. baltica) do not give the same recovery.
No matched abundance controls for every noncognate partner were established;
failed complementation is not proof of intrinsic incompatibility or Leg preference.
Only the carrier MR-1 Maf2 is assigned PF01973 in these cross-partner graphs.
