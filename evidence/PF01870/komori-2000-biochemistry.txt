Figure3: 10 nM asymmetric4Jhs substrate is cleaved by1 uM Pfu Hjc at56 C1 h,
then phenol extracted/ethanol precipitated. Subsequent T4 DNA ligase treatment
at16 C2 h in50 mM Tris pH7.9,10 mM MgCl2,20 mM DTT,1 mM ATP,60 ug/ml BSA
produces a70-nt band from the37-nt cleavage product, absent without ligase.
Starting labeled strand is59 nt; a70-nt marker is displayed. This supports
ligation-compatible cleavage products, not Hjc-catalyzed ligation or a measured
intracellular repair rate. The source does not give a ligase unit dose here.

Figure4B: 100 nM4Jh and10 nM Hjc,56 C30 min; adding MgCl2 relative to zero
added Mg increases resolved product, with optimum around5-10 mM. The substrate,
enzyme and other buffer components remain necessary co-inputs. Figure4C also
shows lesser Mn-supported cleavage and no detected cleavage with Ca or Zn in
the tested protocol. Text calls the zinc salt ZnCl2, caption/image ZnSO4:
retain the inconsistency, not a fabricated counterion resolution.
Figure4E has an apparent reaction optimum70-75 C; reduction above80 C may
reflect substrate melting. Separate enzyme pre-heating experiments cannot be
used to claim that this reaction-temperature curve measures denaturation.

Figure5A: 10 nM labeled4Jb with100 nM Hjc,20 mM Tris-acetate pH8,1 mM DTT,
0.1 mg/ml BSA,5% glycerol,ice10 min. Unlabeled4Jb at0.1,0.5,1 uM decreases
labeled shifted-complex signal; linear Db2 at matched competitor doses does not.
No added Mg in binding buffer;6% native gel contains TGE with2 mM EDTA.
This is competitive binding, not measured DNA cleavage or evidence that binding
causes activation. The separate enzyme titration estimates apparent Kd60 nM.

Figure2 high-enzyme mapping detects cleavage of three-way/loop structures;
the original Figure2 image was not inspected in this session. Do not claim
absolute HJ exclusivity. Figure7 substrate geometry and Figure6 MBP/proteolysis
remain uncurated. An activity peak in fractionation is not proof that no other
resolvase exists in this organism.
