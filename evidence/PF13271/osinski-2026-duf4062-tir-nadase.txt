--- Abstract
DUF4062 is a widespread family of TIR-like NADases that hydrolyze NAD

--- Results: DUF4062 is a new family of TIR-like domains
a conserved Glu as a likely catalytic residue

--- Results: Bacterial DUF4062 mediates antiphage defense
Phage defense required the active site E97 of K. pneumoniae DUF4062 and its auxiliary domain

NAD+ levels were reduced upon phage infection in cells expressing WT KpSwz, but not the E97A mutant

--- Results: KpSwz is a portal-activated NADase
this activity required the active-site residue E97 and portalBas14

--- Results: Human TEP1 is an active NADase
Only TEP1 hydrolyzed NAD+ and NADP+, producing ADP-ribose or ADP-ribose phosphate (ADPRP) and nicotinamide (NAM) with activity dependent on the active site E1008

The isolated DUF4062 domain (TEP1886–1113, hereafter TEP1TIR) was sufficient to hydrolyze NAD+ and NADP+

apparent Km of ~6.9 mM for NADP+ and undeterminable for NAD+

the DUF4062 domain lies adjacent to a NACHT ATPase

--- Results: TEP1 is active in the RNA vault
Purified vaults containing TEP1, but not the E1008A mutant, displayed NADase and NADPase activities

--- Curator notes (not from the source)
- The bacterial experiments used five selected DUF4062 proteins lying in predicted
  defence islands, chiefly the Klebsiella pneumoniae protein the authors name
  Swarozyc (KpSwz). The exact sequences and accessions are given in the preprint's
  supplementary material, which was not inspected here, so no accession is asserted
  for KpSwz and its E97 cannot be placed on a Pfam interval from this extract.
- Of four human DUF4062-NACHT proteins assayed (NWD1, NWD2, NPHP3, TEP1) only TEP1
  hydrolysed NAD+/NADP+. Catalytic activity is therefore not uniform across proteins
  carrying this domain.
- The reported TEP1 kinetics are slow, with an apparent Km near 6.9 mM for NADP+ and
  undeterminable for NAD+, and specific activity rising with enzyme concentration.
  A physiological turnover rate is not established.
- In the human proteins the NTP-hydrolysing NACHT domain is a separate domain
  adjacent to DUF4062, not part of DUF4062 itself.
