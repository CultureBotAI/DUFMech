Figure4A-B: purified MntA, unmodified HepT, ATP and Mg support AMP
addition. The full condition is one input, not independent sufficient causes.
MntA carries an extra24 N-terminal residues including His6. Reaction:
10ug MntA,40ul,20mM Tris,10mM MgCl2,5mM DTT,0-12uM ATP;
30min room temperature, then20ug HepT,60min37C. The3uM ATP reaction
was EDTA-stopped; LC-MS resolves one-,two-,three-AMP products. HepT is
the acceptor, not the transferase. Y104 structural/chemical assignment is
supported by other Results, not a claimed direct movie of sequential steps.

Figure5A: modified HepT (>90% purity after coexpression and second
elution) cleaves ompA RNA less than unmodified preparation at0-160nM,
37C30min. Residual MntA and preparation differences remain qualifications;
the result is a preparation-specific activity comparison, not zero residual
activity or proof of the proposed active-cleft occlusion geometry.
Figure5B-C: in BL21 pET28b constructs, HepT Y104A or partner MntA
D39E/D41E lowers growth/CFU protection relative to wild-type coexpression.
The mutant MntA can still bind; binding alone is not sufficient protection.
Strong HepT-alone versus native-operon RBS differences confound absolute
toxicity comparisons. MntA helix deletion is a further distinct perturbation.

Figure7B-C: CL1_0071 HepT and CL1_0070 MntA were expressed in E. coli
BL21, not native T. cleftensis. HepT Y82F and MntA G33A/S34T diminish
the modified band and PDE-released AMP. The AMP count is not established
for this archaeal member; do not transfer the Shewanella trimer assignment.
Figure7D,0.5mM IPTG at OD6000.1,three cultures: HepT reduces CFU,
MntA coexpression raises them, and either substitution weakens protection.
Rescue is relative, not necessarily full restoration of vector growth.
Mutation effects do not exclude changes in protein folding or abundance.
