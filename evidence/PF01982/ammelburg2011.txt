Source identity and dependence
The university identifies this as a 2011 dissertation. Page 38 assigns the
MJ0056 biochemical work to Ammelburg and links it to bibliography item 4
(page 139), PMID:18073108, doi:10.1016/j.str.2007.09.027. This is a primary
author account of that work, not independent replication or a verified identical
copy of the 2007 journal article. The latter full text remains uninspected.
PDF/printed page numbers agree. Methods pages 49-51, results 55-62, Figure 9
(page 56), Figure 11 (page 60) and Figure 12 (page 63) were inspected; the
figures were also viewed as rendered images, not only text extraction.

A1. Purified kinase observation; pages 49-50, Figure 9D/E, page 57.
Quote: Formation of FMN
Protocol facts: pET-30b MJ0056, E. coli C41(DE3)RIL, IPTG induction; MonoQ,
SP Sepharose, 80 C/20 min heating; the colorless fraction was gel-filtered.
Reaction: 40 mM Tris/HCl pH 8, 50 mM NaCl, 5 mM MgCl2, 1 mM DTT,
50 uM riboflavin, 3 mM nucleotide, 1 uM enzyme, 60 min. Temperatures:
25, 37, 50, 70, 85 C. Figure 9D/E shows the CTP assay at 50 C. Positive/
negative ESI-MS and RF/FMN standards support FMN production. Complete conversion
is the author's interpretation, not an independently recalculated quantitative
yield. Methods describe enzyme-minus controls; Figure 9B/C are standards,
not demonstrated matched enzyme-minus traces. CDP was not separately quantified.

A2. ATP/GTP contrasts; Figure 9F/G and page 57.
Quote: did not yield significant amounts of FMN
The text reports much less FMN with ATP/GTP than with CTP across the tested
temperatures. Figure 9G nevertheless labels a small peak at 455.11 as FMN-H,
while its caption describes absent product. Preserve this internal discrepancy:
neither literal zero turnover nor a detection limit follows from these panels.
No GTP spectrum, replicate error or donor-purity measurement was found in this
inspected section. This is not a claim about uninspected supplements or raw data.

A3. UTP contrast; page 57.
Quote: supports product formation
UTP is reported to support FMN production about an order of magnitude less
efficiently than CTP. No UTP spectrum or fitted rate constant is supplied here.
The graph records the reported direction, not a measured tenfold kinetic ratio.

A4. Ordered binding model; pages 59-61 and Figure 11.
Quote: sequential substrate binding cycle
The comparison is apo NMR 2P3M versus MgCDP complex 2VBU and MgCDP/FMN complex
2VBV, not observed CTP/substrate binding trajectories. The proposed cycle places
nucleotide-associated alpha0 formation before riboflavin binding and second-loop
closure. Failed flavin-only crystallization does not establish impossible binding.
Mg is an assay input and a structural ion, not a tested necessity: this section
does not report metal-removal/readdition experiments. Dimerization, evolutionary
clustering and wHTH gene context are not represented as causal activity edges.

A5. Proposed catalytic base; page 60, supported by contacts discussed on page 62.
Quote: functioning as a base
The author proposes E107-mediated activation of the terminal riboflavin hydroxyl
from the position of E107 relative to FMN oxygen atoms in the product structure.
No residue perturbation, folding control, proton-transfer observation or
time-resolved phosphotransfer is described here. This remains a contextual
hypothesis; current Q60365 sequence2 calls the corresponding residue E103.
See ribk-residue-mapping.txt for exact sequence-coordinate evidence.

Contrasts and remaining uncertainties
The 2008 preparation, buffer, donor/substrate concentrations, added Mg, reaction
time and temperature differ. None is proven to explain its ATP/GTP residuals.
Do not pool the assays or transfer CTP exclusivity to all family members.
The PDF prints ml for two unusually large preparation/reservoir volumes on
page 50. No silent unit correction is made; those volumes are not graph inputs.
The original journal text, unshown controls/spectra, exact historical physical
construct and native physiology still require further evidence.
