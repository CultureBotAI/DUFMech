Figure 7D: 3:1 POPC/POPG liposomes, pH 6 inside and initially pH 8 outside,
100 mM KCl on both sides; 100 mM MES inside, 0.5 mM MOPS outside.
External 50 uM ethidium causes proton efflux, measured by external pH.
DCCD-inactivated EmrE and empty liposomes are controls. Similar total
release at 1:500 and 1:1000 protein:lipid ratios argues against binding-only
release; valinomycin subsequently permits additional flux.

Figure 7F/G: pH about 7.5, 50 mM MOPS inside and 0.05 mM outside.
Positive-inside preparations have 1 mM KCl/99 mM NaCl inside and 100 mM KCl
outside; negative-inside preparations reverse that gradient. Valinomycin
is 1 ug/mL. With ethidium added after valinomycin, positive-inside WT
releases protons; negative-inside WT takes them up. E14Q controls lack
the corresponding flux. The neutral-potassium comparison is retained.
Microelectrode signals are calibrated with acid/base additions; Figure 7H
reports SD and n=3. Assay temperature, F/G-specific ethidium dose and
exact F/G protein:lipid ratio were not resolved from the main text.

Curation interpretation: proton direction is observed; drug movement,
microscopic stoichiometry and a unique symport cycle are not independently
measured by the pH trace. The authors discuss alternative mixed-stoichiometry
paths. No strict universal 2:1 cycle or rapid drug-free leak is asserted.
The supplement URL returned HTML rather than PDF; raw traces, its tables
and clone sequences were not reanalyzed.
