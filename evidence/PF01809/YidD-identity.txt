PF01809 YidD identity and construct assessment
Assessed: 2026-10-11 UTC. Curator: Codex.

Frozen identity is PF01809/YidD, EX_DUF, IPR002696. Yu 2011 identifies
the historical family as DUF37. Its Figure 1B caption instead says DUF31;
this discrepancy does not remap the record to the unrelated DUF31 family.

The cysteines are C31, C37 and C64 in this reference. Their conservation
or cross-linking potential is not a residue-specific catalytic experiment.

Yu 2011 uses MC4100 genomic DNA for YidD expression constructs,
MC4100-A for expression/localization, and MC4100(DE3) for the deletion.
The His-tag forward primer translates as MHHHHHHGSMAPPLS at its coding
start, consistent with an N-terminal tag/linker followed by YidD.
The GFP-H1 construct includes the first 81 coding bases, residues 1-27.
Its reverse-primer annealing segment encodes RLISPLL, matching 21-27.
Thus H1 contains only PF01809 residues 10-27 plus the native 1-9 flank,
not an isolated complete PF01809 domain. Table/figure show GFP N-terminal
to YidD; a Methods sentence says fused 3' to the first bases. Complete
linker primers are not all listed in Table 1. Retain the reported
GFP-YidD/H1 constructs without inventing a fully verified fusion sequence.

Geng 2015 thesis Chapter 4 uses BW25113 Keio yidD::kan deletion cells.
Its pEK746 encodes N-terminally His-tagged YidD, made from pBAD30YidD;
the N-terminal primers agree with the reference start. The complete
historical plasmid sequence and pBAD donor provenance are not independently
verified. Methods and insertion Figure 6 identify Lemo21(DE3) for the
overexpression vesicles, while the Results prose says BL21(DE3). The
co-purification experiments explicitly use BL21(DE3) and are distinct.

These bridges identify the experimental gene/member and current domain,
not resequencing of MC4100, BW25113 or every historical plasmid. Whole-gene
deletions, full-protein expression and partial-sequence GFP fusions do not
demonstrate isolated-domain insertase activity. YidC is a different protein.
