Preparation and causal boundary
BW25113 and Keio yidD::kan cells supply genotype-comparison vesicles;
Figure 4 shows two independent deletion isolates. Default vesicles derive
from LB cultures at 37 C, harvested at OD600 about 0.6. Overnight deletion
cultures receive 25 ug/ml kanamycin; matched antibiotic exposure is not
independently established for every subsequent assay.
Deleting yidD slightly lowers downstream YidC abundance. pTrcyidC,
25 uM IPTG, 30 C growth with 100 ug/ml ampicillin provides mild YidC
expression; pTrc99A is its vector control. S2 shows restored YidC signal.
This is YidC compensation, not same-gene YidD complementation.
All retained effects are INDIRECT with respect to the YidD mechanism:
they alter genotypes or vesicle preparations, not purified YidD alone.

Figure 2: energization-dependent fluorescence quenching
Buffer A: 50 mM HEPES-KOH pH 8, 50 mM KCl, 1 mM DTT, 5 mM MgCl2.
For the delta-pH proxy: 1-ml reactions at 30 C, 12.5 ug/ml vesicles,
1 uM ACMA, 0.625 mM NADH or 1 mM ATP. For the delta-psi proxy:
100 ug/ml vesicles, oxonol VI, 1.25 mM NADH or 1 mM ATP. The inspected
Methods do not specify oxonol concentration. ACMA uses 411/474 nm and
oxonol 523/624 nm excitation/emission. Valinomycin and nigericin at 1 uM
interconvert/collapse the components in separate traces.
Deletion-derived vesicles show smaller fluorescence quenching for both
energy sources with both dyes. Larger raw fluorescence is not a larger
gradient. The graphs retain quenching amplitudes as gradient proxies,
not calibrated pH units, membrane voltages or a direct YidD proton pump.
The plotted traces do not establish replicate variance or membrane leak
versus changed respiratory/ATPase capacity independently.

Figure 3: three distinct biochemical readouts of genotype-derived vesicles
Ferricyanide reduction: 80 ug/ml vesicles, 25 C, 50 mM potassium phosphate
pH 7.5, 5 mM MgSO4, 1 mM ferricyanide and 1 mM NADH; absorbance 420 nm.
The deletion preparation retains about half WT activity.
NADH oxidation with oxygen: 75 ug/ml vesicles, 50 mM HEPES-KOH pH 7.5,
350 uM NADH, absorbance 340 nm. The Results describe about 30% residual
activity; the figure supports a substantial reduction, not a precise
independently re-fitted ratio or exclusive assignment to one respiratory
complex. Ferricyanide and oxygen assays are not interchangeable.
ATP hydrolysis: 15 ug/ml vesicles, 50 mM HEPES-KOH pH 8, 50 mM KCl,
2.5 mM MgCl2, 2 mM DTT, 1 mM ATP, 37 C; phosphate measured at 660 nm
by malachite green. Methods mention 15 min plus stopping at various
intervals. The DCCD-sensitive component is reduced about 40%; remaining
DCCD-insensitive activity is similar. DCCD is 0.5 mM from ethanol stock;
no independent solvent-control reconciliation. Three independent
measurements, mean/SD, are stated for Figure 3.
Neither a proton-pumping defect nor a specific F1-on-F0 assembly step
is directly resolved by these bulk activity measurements.

Figure 4: membrane protein abundance
Deletion-derived vesicles show reduced NuoK and slightly reduced YidC,
with a weak increase in PspA. LepB is similar. F0c is reported as little
affected, unlike Yu 2011's MC4100(DE3) result. Different backgrounds and
preparations prevent treating this as the same experiment or universal
contradiction. No raw densitometry or statistical significance was re-fitted.

Figures 6 and 7: coupled synthesis/protease-protection readouts
The primary experiment inherits additional recipe details from cited
methods, not fully reconstructed here. Buffer is 50 mM HEPES-KOH pH 7.6,
37 C with T7 polymerase and radiolabeled protein mix; 30 min for NuoK,
20 min for F0c. Final IMV dose and translation-extract recipe require
the cited protocol. NuoK samples are pelleted through 20% sucrose,
then exposed to 0.5 mg/ml proteinase K, 30 min on ice. F0c receives
2.5 mg/ml proteinase K, 20 min at room temperature. TCA precipitation,
16% tricine SDS-PAGE and phosphorimaging detect protected material.
NuoK readout is an N-terminal protected fragment after C-terminal cleavage;
F0c readout is protected full-length protein. Buffer-only reactions and
4%/3% synthesis samples respectively control digestion and production.
Protection supports insertion under this protocol, not an absolute measure
of correct assembly, calibrated topology for every molecule or direct
YidD catalytic activity. No detergent-plus-protease control is displayed
in these panels. The no-vesicle controls argue against gross intrinsic
protease resistance but do not exclude every preparation confounder.

Figure 6B/C: BW25113 deletion reduces protected NuoK to roughly half WT
with empty vector. The defect persists in the pTrcyidC background despite
restored YidC abundance; compare matched pTrcyidC WT/deletion bars rather
than calling restoration of YidC restoration of YidD. Five independent
experiments, mean/SD. Figure 6 actual E/F (caption D/E) shows increased
NuoK protection with pEK746 His-YidD overexpression versus pET15b.
Results describe about 50% enhancement; the plotted bar is somewhat above
150%. Only qualitative increase is retained. Methods/Figure identify
Lemo21(DE3), while Results calls the host BL21(DE3). It is not a rescue
of BW25113 deletion. Expression Methods use 30 C growth, induction at
OD600 about 0.5, 500 uM IPTG for 2 h. Historical clone sequence is unverified.
NuoK Results say two transmembrane segments but the schematic shows three;
the graph does not adopt that unverified topology description.

Figure 7B/C: deletion with empty vector reduces F0c protection about 20%.
Mild YidC expression in the deletion restores it to approximately WT
levels; it is not evidence that YidD is universally required for F0c
insertion. Figure 7D/E shows little effect of His-YidD overexpression
on F0c protection; the small plotted difference is not promoted to a
new directional claim. Three independent experiments, mean/SD.
Keep steady-state F0c abundance, protected insertion and DCCD-sensitive
ATPase activity as different endpoints. No observed linear chain links
all three to a specific molecular assembly event.
