Paraphrased assay ledger, pp8804-8807, Expression and Purification,
Enzymatic Studies, Characterization of the TM1643 Dehydrogenase,
Figure5A-C/legend and TableII:
Full-length N-terminal His6 TM1643 was affinity purified after E. coli
expression. The amino-acid assay monitored A340 increase at 70C using
50 mM diethanolamine pH9.8, 5 mM amino acid and 1 mM NAD+ or NADP+.
L-Asp supported cofactor reduction; D-Asp, L-Glu and Asn did not.
The screen used amino-acid, organic-acid and alcohol pools; specific positives
are not evidence of exhaustive substrate specificity across the family.
Figure5 and TableII separately report NAD- and NADP-dependent kinetics.
TableII L-Asp Km values are 0.067+/-0.008 mM with NAD and
1.20+/-0.05 mM with NADP. These conditional values do not prove the
physiological cofactor, domain sufficiency or a cellular NAD-production flux.

The reverse assay monitored A340 decrease at 70C in a 1-ml reaction:
50 mM Tris-HCl pH8.0, 100 mM ammonium sulfate, 0.1 mM EDTA,
0.15 mM NADH or NADPH, 5 mM oxaloacetate and enzyme. The paper reports
both donors active. This is NOT 100 mM NH4Cl and is not the oxidative
pH9.8 assay. No direct product-specific L-Asp quantification is reported
for this reverse reaction in the inspected text; retain the redox readout.

L-malate and ammonium are reported as competitive inhibitors of oxidative
activity, Ki 4.02+/-0.48 and 32.5+/-4.9 mM respectively. No inhibitor
curve, matching counterion control, exact inhibitor-specific donor assignment,
or binding experiment was inspected. L-Glu, succinate, fumarate, pyruvate,
tartrate and D-malate did not affect the activity under the reported tests.
These are scoped biochemical rate effects, not cellular inhibition claims
or direct visualization of substrate-site occupancy. Ammonium inhibition
in this direction must not erase its use in the different reverse assay.

Product/interpretation boundary, pp8805 and8807, Figure2/legend and
Possible Role for TM1643 in NAD Biosynthesis:
The immediate iminoaspartate product is inferred, not directly identified.
Ammonium was detected near-equimolar to NADH via a coupled enzyme assay;
pyruvate was detected by two coupled assays (data not shown). Oxaloacetate
was NOT detected. The authors infer unstable-imine hydrolysis followed by
oxaloacetate decarboxylation under pH9.8/70C conditions. Do not represent
TM1643 itself as an observed pyruvate-producing decarboxylase.
The NAD-biosynthetic assignment relies on biochemistry and genomic context.
A TM1643-NadA channeling complex is proposed, not demonstrated here.
His193 general acid/base catalysis, a mobile 212-219 loop and substrate-induced
closure are structural proposals without residue perturbation proof here.
Asp oxidase, ASA-DH, homoserine dehydrogenase and inositol-phosphate synthase
tests were negative in the stated source scope; several underlying data were
not shown. No gene knockout/rescue or family-wide functional census was read.
