Author-deposited published paper: all main text read; Figures 2, 3, 4, 6
and Table 1 visually inspected. Minimal quotations: "Lys-81";
"chromophoric adduct"; "inorganic phosphate".

Methods p6099: Pdx1-His6 and its K81R/K149R derivatives are whole proteins,
not isolated PF01680 domains. Free Pdx1-His6 is prepared with 0.5 mM DL-G3P
and 20 mM ammonium sulfate for 3 h at room temperature, then dialyzed into
20 mM Tris-Cl pH7.5/20 mM NaCl. This clearance step is not R5P loading.

Figures 3/4, pp6100-6101: R5P exposure produces a covalent early adduct.
Radiolabeled R5P identifies a tryptic fraction; natural/13C-labeled peptide
masses differ by five Da. MS/MS assigns modification to K81 in residues
61-83, rather than the earlier K149 assignment. Figure 4 preincubates R5P
for 3 h at 37 C: WT and K149R each acquire approximately 212.6 Da, whereas
K81R does not show the new mass peak. Figure-specific concentrations are
not stated there. Neither a folding control nor a universal zero-activity
conclusion is supplied by those spectra. The 2007 imine interpretation
is not adopted as settled chemistry: see the retained 2008 NMR follow-up.

Figure 2A: 20 uM free Pdx1, 20 uM Pdx2, 0.2 mM R5P, 10 mM glutamine;
the 315-nm signal rises across 0, 1.5, 3, 4.5, 6, 7.5 and 9 minutes.
Reconstitution Methods use 50 mM Tris-Cl pH7.5; reaction temperature is
not restated there. Do not import 37 C from the separate PLP kinetic assay.
Pdx2 alone or glutamine alone does not support the rise, according to the
text. The inset's Pdx2-His6 H170N trace stays near baseline relative to WT.
This is a partner mutation, not a mutation in PF01680. External ammonium
can replace Pdx2/glutamine; the reported tenfold rate difference is data
not shown, so no quantitative edge is assigned to it.

Figure 2C main panel: first prepare the chromophore as in 2A, then add
0.4 mM DL-G3P; spectra at 3/9/15/25/40/60 min show A315 decreasing and
A414 increasing. The product-associated signal is assigned to PLP, but this
particular sample is not independently identified by product MS/NMR here.
The separate inset dialyzes excess R5P/glutamine and states 0.4 uM DL-G3P,
inconsistent with the main-panel mM dose. The caption ends with pH8.0 while
reconstitution Methods use pH7.5. Preserve that ambiguity rather than
silently fixing units or combining preparations. The inset shows partial,
not quantitative, conversion. The two observed signals do not establish
every intervening bond change or distinguish enzyme-bound from free PLP.

Figure 6C/D and Methods: joint Pdx1/Pdx2, glutamine and R5P yield a coupled
inorganic-phosphate signal at 360 nm, alongside the 315-nm time course.
The phosphate assay uses 300 ul, 50 mM Tris-Cl pH8.0, 20 mM MgCl2 and
EnzChek components; figure curves use equimolar 10/20/40 uM proteins.
Glutamine/R5P doses and temperature are not explicitly enumerated for this
assay. Do not transfer the 2A concentrations. The paper describes release
as stoichiometric, but no precise completed one-to-one yield is assigned
from these finite time courses. Chromophore formation and phosphate release
are concomitant readouts, not proof that one measured signal causes the other.

Figure 6A/B/Table 1 separately fractionate adduct species and assess protein
phosphostaining. Text and table differ on species-a mass (33049 vs 33047 Da).
The nitrogen-free C5H6O2 structure and absence of 15N incorporation rely in
part on data not shown. Later isotope/NMR work challenges this interpretation;
neither that formula nor the proposed ring-opening/tautomerization sequence
is encoded as an observed edge. K149R has a slower chromophore signal and
no detected PLP in text-only results; it is not simply inactive in all steps.
