Product assay (Figure 1D/E): full-length recombinant SaOatA, 5 uM, plus 1 mM
acetyl-CoA and either 1 mM pentaacetyl-chitopentaose (G5) or 10 ug/ml
semisynthetic GM-pentapeptide oligomers; 50 mM sodium phosphate pH6.5, 18 h,
QTOF-MS. Oligomers span 4-10 repeats; products are mutanolysin-digested before
GM-penta analysis. G5 peaks include substrate1034.41 and acetyl-product1076.42
[M+H]+ (also1098.40 sodium adduct); GM-penta967.4 becomes1009.4. Enzyme-free
controls lack these product peaks. Published S453A preparations lack product
in both panels. The isolated C-domain negative comparator is plotted in E,
not D; the Results also describe a G5 C-domain negative experiment. Graph
edges use the displayed controls. No isotope-resolved flux, atomic transition
state or isolated-PF01757 catalysis follows from these net product assays.
The MS-search construct caveat in ms-deposit-caveats.txt is retained for the
published S453A comparator, without asserting that all preparations shared
the extra substitutions. Exact detergent recipe is not established here.

CoASH and G5 assays (Figure 3C and in-vitro Results): relative esterase rates
with acetyl-CoA donor, WT100%; R25A18.4%; R25K178%; H36A0.64%; R86A22.1%;
Y136A155%; E154A4.76%; Y206A no detectable activity. WT specific rate is
28.8 +/-0.20 nmol/min/mg; Y136A44.6 +/-0.48 (not +/-0.038). G5 transfer is
positive for WT,R25A,R25K,R86A,E154A; negative for H36A,Y136A,Y206A. Residual
G5 product in R25A/R86A/E154A is described as low in Results/S7, but the
unseen S7 spectra are not quantitatively reanalyzed. H36A retains detectable
CoASH release despite no G5 product; Y136A increases that release while losing
G5 product. These assays must not be collapsed into a single activity value.
Figure3C: 50 mM sodium phosphate pH7, 5% DMSO,25 C,0.1 mM acetyl-CoA and
0.2 mM DTDP; transfer assays include1 mM G5. This caption differs from
Figure1D/E's pH6.5/1 mM donor recipe. The Figure3C transfer endpoint time and
the exact application of the DTDP mixture remain uncertain without SI.
S453A has5.4% relative release but was assayed in5% ethanol instead of DMSO;
it is not used as a solvent-matched rate edge. Figure3D labels R25A in the
caption while the Results and kinetic values describe R25K; do not assign
that fitted kinetic curve to R25A. Y136A was not saturated with donor.
Purified variants were produced in C43(DE3) and isolated by IMAC with fresh
resin for each. Apparent purity is stated; FigureS2 gels remain uninspected.
Rate differences alone do not resolve folding versus catalytic chemistry.

Cellular PG (Figure1B, Results and PG-isolation Methods): base-labile acetyl
groups normalized to muramic acid in purified PG, three replicates and SD.
USA300 WT about40%; markerless oatA deletion undetectable; deletion carrying
WT pACCJ3 about35%. H36A complementation has no detectable PG acetylation;
Y206A about1.3%; R83A about29%. The graph keeps deletion/rescue and H36A/Y206A
effects separate from purified-protein results. Figure3A membrane anti-His
blots show roughly comparable detected expression of selected impaired
variants, not proof of identical folding, trafficking or stability. Do not
apply the tunicamycin growth recipe to PG isolation without further evidence.

Growth (Figure1A, lysozyme Results and in-situ Methods): USA300 deletion
versus WT, and deletion plus WT pACCJ3 complementation, with0.4 ug/ml
tunicamycin and0.25-4 mg/ml hen egg-white lysozyme, TSB,37 C,1% DMSO.
Plotted endpoint12 h relative delta-OD600, n3 with SD; Methods monitor20 h
at20-min intervals with double-orbital shaking, not an18-h viability assay.
Deletion has no OD increase at1 mg/ml and higher; WT/complement retain
substantial growth even at4 mg/ml. Without tunicamycin the main text describes
a growth lag, not complete death (S1 not inspected). Figure1A gray empty-vector
caption names USA300+pALC2073 whereas Results describe deletion+empty vector.
Edges use the unambiguous WT/deletion/complement curves, not a silently
corrected gray-curve genotype. No bactericidal endpoint or direct
PG-acetylation-to-growth mediation experiment is claimed.

Mechanism (Figure5, Discussion, trapping Results and Figure4): the authors
propose H36/E154-assisted Tyr206 acylation and a Tyr206-to-Ser453 acetyl relay.
Both steps remain HYPOTHESIZED in the native graph. Purified preparation
product/rate effects do not directly observe these intramolecular transfers.
Intact-protein LC-MS failed to resolve treated adducts. MALDI signals were
broad, displaced by about4000Da, with mean treatment shift124.9Da; this is not
a reliable count of three acetyl groups. Peptide acetylation atY206 andS453
is relevant but not a treatment-specific flux measurement; deposited Y206
control and construct differences are documented separately. Figure5A uses
an acetyl-CoA conformation from a different enzyme, not an OatA complex.
Topology combines fusion readouts with predictions, not an atomic structure.

partner-only assay is contextual evidence for, not observation of, the
