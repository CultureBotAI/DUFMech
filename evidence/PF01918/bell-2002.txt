Paraphrased assay ledger:
Figure 3 recombinant S. solfataricus Alba binds a 50-bp DNA duplex.
Apparent Kd (nM, triplicate standard errors): WT 33+/-10;
K16A 220+/-18; K16E 1300+/-280; K17A 480+/-90;
K17E 1400+/-300. Native preparation 1100+/-330.
These are affinity losses (higher Kd), despite confusing reduced-Kd wording
elsewhere. Point mutants do not by themselves establish acetylation chemistry.
Native-versus-recombinant preparations differ beyond any single modification.

Figure 4A/B: recombinant Alba 1/3/10 ug reduces full-length transcription in
a reconstituted SSV1 T6 system; matched Sso7d lacks that repression.
The native preparation, called Ac-Alba, does not repress similarly.
Figure 4C assays K17A and K16E, not all four Figure 3 substitutions.
Figure 4D Sir2 +/- NAD on naked DNA has no detectable repression.
Figure 4E joint native Alba, WT Sir2 (1 ug) and NAD (200 uM) lowers RNA
relative to native Alba alone, H116Y Sir2 or minus-NAD controls.
Preincubation 20 min at 65 C precedes NTP addition to 200 uM.
The original figure confirms microM, which PDF extraction can corrupt.
This is a coupled transcription endpoint, not direct measurement of a
deacetylation product or an in-vivo spreading-silencing trajectory.
The original native-lysine-acetylation interpretation is challenged by
Cao2018, PMID:29679495; preserve the assay result without adopting that model.
