Figure 3B/C: recombinant human SBDS and EFL1 together with GTP redistribute
eIF6 from bound to free fractions of pre-60S particles from Sbds-deleted mouse
liver. Individual-factor controls do not release eIF6; Ebp1/Nmd3 stay bound.
GDP/no nucleotide fail, GDPNP permits marginal release. No precise release
fraction or temporal ordering of molecular conformations is measured.
Release assay: 100 ul; about 0.5-1 pmol mouse pre-60S, 5 pmol each SBDS/EFL1,
100 uM nucleotide; 10 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.4, 75 mM KCl, 1.5 mM MgCl2,
protease/RNase inhibitors; 10 min at 37 C. A 30% sucrose cushion separates
free/bound fractions for immunoblotting after centrifugation at 4 C.
Figure 3D is a separate system: 5 pmol salt-washed rabbit reticulocyte 60S,
preloaded 20 min on ice with excess recombinant human eIF6(1-225), with free
eIF6 removed through a 100-kDa filter. SBDS/EFL1/GTP release this truncation;
Rpl28 remains bound, GDP is ineffective and EDTA is a positive control.
The result excludes a requirement for the omitted S235 in this assay, not all
phosphorylation-dependent regulation or a direct measurement of 80S assembly.

Figure 6B: 50 ul with 5 nmol gamma-32P-GTP, 5 pmol rabbit 60S, 6.3 pmol
SBDS and 40 pmol EFL1; 50 mM Tris pH 7.4, 2 mM MgCl2, 150 mM NaCl,
2 mM DTT; 30 min at 37 C. Charcoal separation and scintillation measure Pi.
WT SBDS increases 60S-dependent EFL1 turnover versus no SBDS. R126T gives
less stimulation than WT. K151N still increases turnover versus no SBDS;
its nonsignificant difference from WT is not proof of kinetic equivalence.
Three experiments with mean/SD are reported for bars; the concentration series
inset uses duplicates. SBDS without 60S does not significantly stimulate EFL1;
EFL1 H96A limits attribution to an unrelated contaminating GTPase.
Figure 6C: R126T and K151N each reduce eIF6 release relative to WT in the
mouse-particle assay. Main-text HSQC evidence describes preserved overall fold
with local R126T perturbation. Supplement S7 binding evidence was not inspected.
Hydrolysis and release use different particles/buffers and separate vessels;
their comparison supports uncoupling, not a single directly filmed reaction.
