Curator paraphrase:
The discovery library used P.furiosus PF0012; the detailed purified-protein
experiments here use T.kodakarensis TK1898/Q5JER9, not that library member.
At55C in Mg-containing reactions, TkoEndoMS cleaves both strands of
G/T-containing45-bp duplexes, yielding a five-nucleotide5-prime overhang
(Figure3). Normal duplex and ssDNA controls are negative at the low
enzyme concentrations of Figure2. Do not transfer that negative to Figure6.

FigureS6 uses equal100nM dimer protein with5nM G/T duplex. Purified
D165A, E179A and K181A proteins show loss of detectable product versus
WT. SDS-PAGE demonstrates recovered protein; it does not independently
establish intact folding. N76D retains activity in this assay. All three
loss substitutions map within PF01939; N76 is in PF21003.

Figure8: adding cognate PCNA raises cleavage by4nM WT dimer, with5nM
G/T substrate. The deltaPIP construct retains baseline cleavage but lacks
the PCNA-dependent increase. The deleted QKTLF248-252 sequence is beyond
the current PF01939 endpoint245. This is not isolated-domain sufficiency
or complete loss of catalysis. PCNA alone is not the nuclease.

Figure6 uses50nM substrate,0-400nM dimer protein,55C10min and D165A
negative controls. Higher concentrations produce cleavage of3-prime/5-prime
flaps,3-prime/5-prime splayed substrates, ordinary duplex and ssDNA.
Patterns are heterogeneous and inefficient compared with mismatch cleavage;
the graph must not turn these high-concentration effects into native
substrate preferences. The same DNA is not claimed to be both uncleavable
and cleavable without retaining the concentration difference.

Mismatch preferences and flanking-base effects are assay-specific. The paper
does not establish a complete native repair chain or universal recombination
requirement. Its2016 repair model is distinct from later cellular findings.
