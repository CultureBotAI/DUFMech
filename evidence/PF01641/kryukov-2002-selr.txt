Read primary Methods, Results, Discussion and captions. Figures 1-4 were visually
inspected; Figure 1 is a sequence alignment and Figure 4 is a pathway model, not
additional causal assays. Figure 2 contains liquid growth curves and plate spots;
Figure 3 contains HPLC chromatograms. No original chromatographic data or
statistical reanalysis was obtained. The article refers to an earlier assay
protocol instead of restating all enzyme/substrate/DTT doses and reaction times.

Drosophila SelR: experimental cDNA AF486578, protein AAM10931.1, 155 residues.
The paper explicitly distinguishes this clone from a predicted 166-aa ORF with
an extra internal 11 residues, used in the alignment. Express N-terminal
His6-tagged protein from pET28a in E. coli BL21(DE3), Talon affinity purification.
Figure 3B compares separate R and S dabsyl-L-methionine-sulfoxide substrates
with and without SelR. An extra dabsyl-methionine product peak appears with the
R substrate, not S, detected at 436 nm. DTT is the reducing assay context, not
an established physiological electron donor here. The 1.5-min peak is identified
as dabsyl-chloride impurity, not product. Dabsylated substrate is a proxy, not an
intact oxidized protein or a demonstrated free-methionine utilization mechanism.

Mouse SelR: full coding region of a Cys-for-Sec mutant cloned into pET21d(+),
expressed in BL21(DE3), purified with DEAE flow-through and heparin HPLC.
Figure 3A shows dabsyl-methionine product with the R substrate and no corresponding
product with S. A matched mouse R-substrate/no-enzyme trace is not displayed in
panel A (the fly controls are separate panel B). The natural Sec-containing
mouse protein was not the tested purified construct, and a mutant-versus-native
activity ratio is not measured. Vector-derived terminal residues were not
independently determined; do not call this a verified tag-free native sequence.
The primary PCR primers match the current reference's terminal coding sequence.

Yeast genetics: purchased BY4741 WT and isogenic SelR::KAN-R / YCL033C mutant;
MsrA deletion GY4 and double deletion GY5 made by URA3 replacement, PCR checked.
Methods prints MAT-alpha for BY4741 with his3D1/leu2D0/met15D0/ura3D0; this
historical mating-type notation is retained as unresolved rather than silently
corrected or used to identify a different strain. Reference P25566 is S288c,
not a resequenced BY4741 experimental isolate. No SelR genetic rescue is shown.

Figure 2A-C: stationary overnight cultures diluted to OD600 0.05 in supplemented
YNB, 30 C, 250 rpm, followed for about 14 h. Exact supplement composition is
not enumerated in the figure caption. Zero peroxide, 2 mM peroxide and 0.5 mM
peroxide are distinct assays. At zero peroxide, SelR-only deletion tracks WT,
whereas the double deletion accumulates biomass more slowly than WT and the
MsrA-only mutant. At 2 mM, SelR-only deletion delays early/mid growth relative
to WT but approaches the final WT OD; the double mutant has no substantial
increase during the displayed interval, unlike MsrA-only deletion. At 0.5 mM,
single mutants largely track WT, while the double mutant grows very poorly.
These are conditional biomass phenotypes, not measured intracellular repair
fluxes or proof that cells cannot grow at any later time. There are no plotted
replicate/error bars or quantified growth-rate fits.

Figure 2D: separate 3-day plate spots, 30 C, 0.7 mM peroxide and 0.14 mM
methionine or R, S or mixed sulfoxides; four inoculum dilutions from OD600 0.2
to 0.0002, 10 ul spots. Both gene deletions affect utilization of both isomers
in the authors' interpretation, with the MsrA effect stronger. These growth
results do not overturn purified stereospecificity or prove direct reduction
of free R substrate by MsrA. Qualitative spot effects are retained as context,
not quantified into extra edges or blended with the liquid assays.

Metal analysis: mouse mutant has 1.08 Zn equivalents as isolated; a separate
preparation after dialysis has 0.33 without EDTA or 0.42 with EDTA; fly protein
has 0.82. Corresponding buffer controls lack detected zinc. Different preparation
and dialysis conditions do not by themselves prove a catalytic zinc requirement.
No causal zinc-removal/activity edge is inferred from Table 2. Native selenium
is part of selenocysteine, not an independently measured bound selenium ligand.
