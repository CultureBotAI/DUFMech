S1 construct: pSL152, T. litoralis chromosomal template, C-terminal His6,
E. coli SF120 expression, 80 C/10 min heat treatment and Ni-NTA purification.
The exact full insert/tag and study strain were not independently established.
S1a, Figure 8 and transcription methods: 300 ng linearized pMLP with the
T. litoralis malE promoter, P. furiosus TBP (200 ng), TFB (25 ng) and RNAP
(110 ng), at 80 C. Buffer: 40 mM HEPES pH 7.3, 250 mM KCl, 2.5 mM MgCl2,
0.1 mM EDTA, 1 mM DTT; ATP/GTP/CTP 0.44 mM each, UTP 0.002 mM plus label.
Duration and total reaction volume were not established from the inspected
text. TrmB 0.8-3.2 ug lowers the 110-nt transcript. The 89-nt gdh transcript
is a transcription-system control, not a full gdh-plus-TrmB specificity series.
With 3.2 ug TrmB, maltose at 100 uM (A8) or trehalose at 2.5 mM (B11)
relieves repression. Prose also says above 2.5 mM for trehalose; retain the
caption's lane condition without claiming a precise universal threshold.
Sugar-only controls at 5 mM and no-regulator controls are reported. No native
transporter flux, binding microstep or isolated-domain activity follows.

S1b, Figure 6 and EMSA methods: 1.25 uM TrmB, labeled intergenic region 1,
25 mM HEPES pH 7.5, 150 mM potassium glutamate, 10% glycerol, 1 mM DTT,
poly(dI-dC) competitor, 70 C/10 min then 8% native PAGE at room temperature.
Maltose 5/10/20/50 uM reduces retardation (reported half-effect 20-50 uM).
Trehalose 1/2.5/5/10 mM does not reproduce maltose's loss of shift; 5-10 mM
increases the shifted/stabilized complex. This is not a measured off-rate or
proof of oligomer stoichiometry. Trehalose cannot be assigned a universal
DNA-dissociation edge from its transcription effect.

S1c, Figure 4 and footprint methods: TrmB 0/1.25/2.5/5 uM, 70 C/10 min
binding, 0.005 U DNaseI at 37 C/5 min. Coding/noncoding strands protect the
BRE/TATA region and part of an inverted repeat; 100 uM maltose with 5 uM
TrmB removes protection. Reported trehalose footprint loss at 2.5 mM is
data-not-shown and is not encoded as a separately inspected figure result.
Protection overlapping BRE/TATA motivates TBP occlusion; it does not measure
competitive TBP displacement. The proposed occlusion edge is HYPOTHESIZED.
Native malE RNA induction with maltose lacks an isolated TrmB perturbation and
is not adopted as a TrmB-specific causal effect. Sugar binding and reported
fluorescence changes are background, not proof of an atomic allosteric path.

OPEN COVERAGE / EXCLUSIONS
Lee2005 PMID16135241 and differential-signals Lee2007 PMID17504272 are leads;
complete methods/figures not independently inspected here. The different 2007
PF0124/TrmBL1 article is not interchangeable with them. Remaining thesis
anti-inducer, promoter-engineering and cross-promoter assays; Tgr/TrmBL1/2/4;
and other taxa remain in scope for future primary-source assessment and mapping.
Public-source access failures are coverage limits, not negative literature
findings. No graph implies all 8,669 source-count proteins share these effects.

or whole-family function is demonstrated by the adopted assays.
