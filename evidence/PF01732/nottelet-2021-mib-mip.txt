Minimal source quotation: "Antigen-bound immunoglobulin cleavage".

Figure 4 compares preassembled goat IgG complexes with human transferrin,
human albumin or HRP. Addition of MIB83 plus WT MIP82 or inactive S759A
MIP82 shifts high-mass aggregates into separated antigen and Ig-containing
fractions. MIB alone also separates antigen. Thus cleavage and MIP itself
are not established necessities for dissociation; no MIP-alone effect is drawn.
SEC protocol: 3 uM IgG, 10 uM antigen, 6 uM each added MIB/MIP, 200 ul PBS.
Results/Methods say 20-min preincubation at room temperature; caption says
30 min. The separate cleavage assay uses 2/10/5/5 uM in 15 ul, not SEC doses.
Constructs lack MIB residues 1-35 / MIP 1-33 and carry N-terminal His tags.

Figure 5C/E/F separately compares cellular WT, deletion of all four MIB-MIP
pairs, and a strain retaining MIB83-MIP82. Deletion reduces extracellular IgG
cleavage with serum IgG and IgM cleavage with D12 immune serum, and increases
D12-serum agglutination. D0 IgM is not detectably cleaved by cells; purified
proteins cleave both D0/D12 samples. The retained pair is not a reintroduction
rescue. Anti-HRP is not cleaved in cells despite purified-system cleavage.
Cleavage: about 1e9 washed cells, 15 ul serum-free SP5 with 100 ng/ul IgG or
2% albumin-depleted serum, 30 min at 37 C; supernatant Western blot.
Agglutination: overnight at 37 C without agitation; Methods give 4% serum,
caption 2%. OD310 is settled aggregate measurement, not a growth curve.

Limits: whole-cell deletion removes binding proteins AND proteases; effects
are indirect and do not isolate MIP82 or PF01732. Main Figure 5B establishes
the locus design, but Table S2 and supplemental strain-validation figures
were inaccessible (publisher supplement HTTP 403). Exact clone background
and breakpoints are not independently sequence-verified here. Results panel
cross-references are offset; use the actual Figure 5C/E/F labels. Quantitative
densitometry, full supplement and all 16 paralog combinations were not reviewed.
No structural contact is promoted to an observed atomic catalytic transition.
