Source description

This presumed domain is functionally uncharacterised. This domain family is found in bacteria, archaea and eukaryotes. This domain shows structural similarity to TIR domains. This domain is found within the TEP1 (Telomerase protein component 1) protein.

The human TEP1 DUF4062 domain hydrolyses NAD+ and NADP+ and depends on Glu1008

MECHANISTIC

Scope
Recombinant full-length human TEP1 (UniProtKB:Q99973), its isolated DUF4062-containing construct TEP1 886-1113, the E1008A substitution, and human vaults reconstituted from MVP plus TEP1. This is one tested protein, not a family-wide property, and not endogenous regulation of TEP1 in cells.
Context
Full-length human NWD1, NWD2, NPHP3 and TEP1 were purified from HEK293F cells, incubated with NAD+ and NADP+, and reaction products analysed by HPLC. Of those four, only TEP1 was active. The isolated TEP1 886-1113 construct was assayed separately. Vaults were reconstituted with MVP and TEP1, or TEP1 E1008A, in HEK293F cells and assayed for NADase and NADPase activity.
Family mapping
UniProtKB:Q99973 is 2627 residues with a single Pfam:PF13271 interval at 901-1015, inclusive. The retrieved sequence confirms the source's numbering, with residue 1008 a glutamate and residues 908, 954 and 976 arginines, all inside that interval. Coordinate limit, the tested catalytic construct TEP1 886-1113 extends 15 residues before and 98 residues after the PF13271 interval, so the measured activity maps to a region containing the DUF4062 domain rather than strictly to the Pfam interval. The NACHT, P-loop and WD40 regions of Q99973 lie outside 901-1015 and are not the object of this result.
Limitations
The sole source is a bioRxiv preprint that has not been peer reviewed. The reported kinetics are slow, with an apparent Km near 6.9 mM for NADP+ and undeterminable for NAD+, and specific activity rising with enzyme concentration, so no physiological turnover rate is established and oligomerisation-dependent activation remains an interpretation. Three of the four human DUF4062-NACHT paralogues assayed were inactive, so this result does not transfer to other family members. No elementary chemical step, proton transfer or substrate-binding geometry is resolved here, and the biological role of the vault is unresolved. Figure panels and raw chromatograms were not inspected; the quotations come from the abstract and results text.

A Klebsiella DUF4062 protein depletes NAD+ on phage portal recognition and aborts infection

MECHANISTIC

Scope
The Klebsiella pneumoniae DUF4062 protein the source names Swarozyc (KpSwz), expressed in Escherichia coli BW25113, with its E97A substitution and its auxiliary domain, challenged with phage Bas14 or co-expressed with the Bas14 portal protein. The tested object is that single protein in a heterologous host. Neither the native Klebsiella context nor other family members are covered.
Context
Five DUF4062 proteins lying in predicted antiphage defence islands were expressed in E. coli BW25113 and challenged with phages from the BASEL library. Liquid cultures expressing KpSwz were infected with phage Bas14 at high and low multiplicity, and cellular NAD+ was measured. Purified KpSwz was incubated with NAD+ with and without the Bas14 portal protein, and products were analysed by HPLC-UV.
Family mapping
The source identifies the tested protein as a DUF4062-containing protein of Klebsiella pneumoniae, recovered in its own DUF4062 family search, and numbers its active-site glutamate E97. Coordinate limit, the sequences and accessions of the five tested bacterial proteins are given only in the supplementary material of the preprint, which was not inspected, so no UniProt or NCBI accession is asserted here and E97 is not placed on a PF13271 interval. The nodes of this graph are therefore deliberately ungrounded, and the domain assignment rests on the family search of the authors rather than on an independently checked match.
Limitations
The sole source is a bioRxiv preprint that has not been peer reviewed. Because the tested sequence is unresolved, the mapping of E97 onto the Pfam domain is the assignment of the authors and was not independently verified. Defence was assayed in a heterologous E. coli host, so the native Klebsiella phenotype is untested. The escaper-mutation and cryo-EM results support portal recognition as the trigger, but the authors describe the final step, repositioning of the TIR-like domains into an active conformation, as a proposal supported by modelling rather than an observed transition, and that step is deliberately absent from this graph. Whether cell death follows from NAD+ loss alone is not resolved. Nothing here transfers to short DUF4062 proteins in other lineages, including cyanobacteria.

    duf4062-ntpase-label-versus-adjacent-nacht
    prompt
    Should a short stand-alone DUF4062 protein be annotated as an NTPase because its orthogroup is labelled a NACHT NTPase?
    kind
    INTERPRETATION
    status
    OPEN
    rationale
    Orthogroup labels such as COG5635, "Predicted NTPase, NACHT family domain", group short stand-alone DUF4062 proteins together with large multidomain relatives in which the NTP-hydrolysing NACHT domain is a separate domain adjacent to DUF4062. Automated pipelines transfer the NTPase label across that boundary. In a UniProtKB snapshot taken on 2026-10-30, a P-loop NTPase domain (IPR027417) co-occurs with DUF4062 in 2514 of 2925 proteins over 450 aa, or 85.9%, but in only 23 of 1720 proteins of 450 aa or less, or 1.3%, and NACHT itself in one. The activity reported for the domain is NAD+ hydrolysis, a different chemistry from NTP hydrolysis. This bears on how DUFMech should treat orthogroup-derived functional labels generally. It is not resolved here, and no NTPase activity has been tested for or excluded in any short member.

    Is a short stand-alone bacterial DUF4062 protein such as Synechococcus elongatus Synpcc7942_0873 an active NADase, and what activates it?
    kind

    status

    rationale
    The tested bacterial proteins were chosen because they lie in defence islands and share a similar C-terminal auxiliary domain, and activation ran through a phage portal protein. UniProtKB:Q31PW6 (Synpcc7942_0873) has the same compact layout, with PF13271 at 6-146 of 362 residues, a predicted coiled coil at 170-197 and an unassigned C-terminal region, and it retains the most conserved glutamate of the family at Glu83. That residue is identical in 93% of 375 short bacterial homologs and sits at Pfam HMM match state 78, whose consensus residue is a highly conserved E with emission probability 0.602. The protein has no Walker A or Walker B motif. None of that is activity. The family is not uniformly catalytic, since three of four human paralogues assayed were inactive, so conservation of the glutamate cannot settle whether this protein is an enzyme, and no causal edge is curated for it. Resolving it needs an assay, not more sequence analysis.

Status and evidence

Seed status
A name-based classification of frozen InterPro/Pfam metadata. UNKNOWN CANDIDATE means the metadata explicitly says the function is unknown. KNOWN HISTORICAL DUF means a DUF name matched without that wording; this heuristic is not proof of a known function and does not establish experimental characterization. EX DUF means Pfam renamed the family away from a DUF/UPF name, as recorded in Pfam's previous identifiers; a rename usually follows published work on some members but is naming history, not proof of function for every member. Some EX DUF families' current names or descriptions still say the function is unknown; their classification basis lists this, and scoring does not treat them as characterized. FALSE POSITIVE TEXT HIT means the naming rules did not match.
Characterization
A separate evidence-scoring result. UNSCORED means no score has been calculated; it is not evidence that the family lacks a known function. Missing scores are not negative evidence. PARTIALLY CHARACTERIZED has partial support; KNOWN HISTORICAL DUF can inherit the seed label or reflect a known-function signal. Neither label implies experimental validation.
Evidence counts
Source-specific signals, not unique proteins, publications, or confidence scores. Known counts Rhea reactions and experimentally supported GO molecular-function annotations. Partial counts functional/domain assignments. Context counts structural, environmental, orthology, interaction and neighborhood signals; context alone does not assign function. Repeated annotations may not be independent.
Curation status
Reported separately from seed classification and characterization. SNAPSHOT ONLY means no curated metadata was supplied for this build. SEEDED denotes a source-derived record, not an expert-reviewed functional claim.
Missing counts
Not available means the source did not supply that counter. A zero is a reported count. Not scored means evidence counts are unavailable.
