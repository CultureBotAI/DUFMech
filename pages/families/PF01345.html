PF01345: CLIPPER domain
IPR001434: integrated entry for CLIPPER domain

Source description

This entry, previously known as DUF11, represents the CLIPPER domain, (Cross-Linked IsoPeptide Protein in the Extracellular Region) which is usually found in multiple copies in a number of archaebacterial proteins. CLIPPER domain adopts an Ig-like fold and contains a conserved N-terminal lysine and a C-terminal asparagine with central aspartate/glutamate residues that form an intramolecular isopeptide bond [InterPro reference PUB00163642]. The positions of the residues predicted to form isopeptide bonds are not universally conserved suggesting that some domains appear to lack the intramolecular isopeptide bonds. CLIPPER domains are found in proteins from Gram-positive and Gram-negative bacteria. Some have also been identified in multiple cell-surface proteins from archaea. In Methanothermobacter sp. CaT2, this domain plays a key role as a membrane-bound adhesion protein in the aggregation of CaT2 [InterPro reference PUB00097782].

Context
Compare WT with K715A by circular dichroism during heating from 5 to 95 C in 5-C increments. Family mapping

Limitations
Both starting spectra resemble folded beta-sheet proteins, but the comparison does not isolate a single atomic cause. Main text/Figure 5 say Asp748, whereas both reference and synthetic sequences encode N748/D749. Methods also say K517A in places despite Table S6 confirming K715A. No catalytic microstep, fitted thermodynamic melting constant, force resistance or native adhesion is asserted.
