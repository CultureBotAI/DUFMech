Source description

This family is the Small Multidrug Resistance (SMR) family. Several members have been shown to export a range of toxins, including ethidium bromide ([InterPro reference PUB00019898] and quaternary ammonium compounds [InterPro reference PUB00002174], through coupling with proton influx [InterPro reference PUB00019899].

Ethidium addition drives EmrE-dependent proton release under a pH gradient

MECHANISTIC

Scope
Reconstituted E. coli EmrE in 3:1 POPC/POPG liposomes, Figure 7D. This is an intact-protein membrane assay, not an isolated Pfam interval or a cellular drug-resistance assay.
Context
Interior 100 mM MES at pH 6; exterior 0.5 mM MOPS adjusted to about pH 8; 100 mM KCl on both sides. External ethidium is 50 uM. The experiment compares 1:500 and 1:1000 EmrE-to-lipid molar ratios at fixed liposome amount, empty liposomes and DCCD-inactivated EmrE. A microelectrode follows external pH; calibrated base additions quantify release. Subsequent valinomycin and protonophore additions are controls, not requirements for the initial release.
Family mapping
UniProtKB:P23895, emrE/b0543/JW0531, is a 110-residue K-12 reference, sequence version 1, with exact UniProt/InterPro sequence agreement and PF00893 at 3-94 inclusive. Reference E14 is within that interval. The paper names E. coli EmrE expressed in BL21(DE3) and cites the Chang pET15b protocol with N-terminal His6 removal leaving GS. Complete 2017 plasmid sequences were not independently verified; reference numbering excludes the GS remnant and K-12 is not the expression-host identity.
Limitations
Proton release is directly measured, whereas drug accumulation and exact microscopic coupling ratios are not independently read out by this pH trace. Similar release at two protein densities argues against binding-only release but does not establish a unique transport cycle. Assay temperature remains unresolved; the unavailable supplement and raw traces were not reanalyzed. This result does not imply universal strict 2H-per-drug antiport.

Positive-inside potential supports outward proton flux through EmrE

MECHANISTIC

Scope
Figure 7F WT versus E14Q E. coli EmrE proteoliposomes after valinomycin then ethidium addition; neutral-potassium WT preparation is a separate comparator within the assay.
Context
3:1 POPC/POPG; pH about 7.5 on both sides, 50 mM MOPS inside and 0.05 mM outside. Inside contains 1 mM KCl and 99 mM NaCl; outside contains 100 mM KCl. Valinomycin at 1 ug/mL generates positive-inside potential. External pH is measured by microelectrode and acid/base calibration. Figure 7H reports SD and n=3; the main text does not resolve panel-specific ethidium dose, temperature or protein-to-lipid ratio within its overall 1:400-1:1000 range.
Family mapping
P23895 full-length reference has PF00893 at 3-94; E14Q changes the mapped glutamate. The 2017 experiment cites the pET15b/His6-cleavage protocol leaving GS at the N terminus. Purification-host BL21(DE3) and K-12 reference identity remain distinct; complete later construct sequences are not established here.
Limitations
The observed variable is proton movement. Its compatibility with electrogenic antiport does not independently fix every drug/proton stoichiometry or prove an elementary proton-transfer role for E14. E14Q is a functional control, not a folding-rescue experiment. The figure does not show rapid drug-free leakage when potential is applied.

Negative-inside potential reverses the net EmrE proton response

MECHANISTIC

Scope
Figure 7G WT and E14Q proteoliposomes with valinomycin added before ethidium. The reversed order-of-addition trace is a separate interpretive control, not the same time course.
Context
3:1 POPC/POPG at pH about 7.5, with 50 mM MOPS inside and 0.05 mM outside. Inside has 100 mM KCl; outside has 1 mM KCl and 99 mM NaCl. Valinomycin at 1 ug/mL establishes negative-inside potential before ethidium addition. A calibrated external-pH microelectrode reports net proton uptake. Exact panel-specific ethidium dose, temperature and protein-to-lipid ratio remain unresolved from the main text.
Family mapping
E. coli EmrE P23895, 110 native residues, sequence version 1; PF00893 interval 3-94 contains native E14. This is an intact reconstituted protein from the cited expression/purification workflow, not an isolated-domain assay or direct measurement in the K-12 reference strain.
Limitations
Proton influx is observed, but a unique simultaneous proton/drug symport path is not. The source discusses mixed import/export stoichiometries as an alternative and acknowledges that the assay cannot definitively distinguish them. Raw traces, supplementary quantitation, folding controls and complete mutant clone sequence were not independently assessed. No universal proton leak or all-SMR mechanism is assigned.

Seed status
A name-based classification of frozen InterPro/Pfam metadata. UNKNOWN CANDIDATE means the metadata explicitly says the function is unknown. KNOWN HISTORICAL DUF means a DUF name matched without that wording; this heuristic is not proof of a known function and does not establish experimental characterization. EX DUF means Pfam renamed the family away from a DUF/UPF name, as recorded in Pfam's previous identifiers; a rename usually follows published work on some members but is naming history, not proof of function for every member. Some EX DUF families' current names or descriptions still say the function is unknown; their classification basis lists this, and scoring does not treat them as characterized. FALSE POSITIVE TEXT HIT means the naming rules did not match.
Characterization
A separate evidence-scoring result. UNSCORED means no score has been calculated; it is not evidence that the family lacks a known function. Missing scores are not negative evidence. PARTIALLY CHARACTERIZED has partial support; KNOWN HISTORICAL DUF can inherit the seed label or reflect a known-function signal. Neither label implies experimental validation.

Repeated annotations may not be independent.
