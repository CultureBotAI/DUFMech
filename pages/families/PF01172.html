Source description

This entry represents the N-terminal domain of proteins that are highly conserved in species ranging from archaea to vertebrates and plants [InterPro reference PUB00020303], including several Shwachman-Bodian-Diamond syndrome (SBDS) proteins from both mouse and humans. Shwachman-Diamond syndrome is an autosomal recessive disorder with clinical features that include pancreatic exocrine insufficiency, haematological dysfunction and skeletal abnormalities. It is characterised by bone marrow failure and leukemia predisposition. Members of this entry play a role in RNA metabolism [InterPro reference PUB00020304][InterPro reference PUB00020305]. In yeast these proteins have been shown to be critical for the release and recycling of the nucleolar shuttling factor Tif6 from pre-60S ribosomes, a key step in 60S maturation and translational activation of ribosomes [InterPro reference PUB00045126]. This data links defective late 60S subunit maturation to an inherited bone marrow failure syndrome associated with leukemia predisposition [InterPro reference PUB00045126].

Joint SBDS, EFL1 and GTP release eIF6 from mouse pre-60S particles

MECHANISTIC

Scope
Recombinant human SBDS and EFL1 acting on eIF6-loaded pre-60S particles isolated from Sbds-deleted mouse liver in Finch 2011 Figure 3B/C. This is a mixed-species, whole-protein reconstitution.
Context
100 ul with about 0.5-1 pmol pre-60S, 5 pmol each SBDS/EFL1, 100 uM GTP, 10 mM Tris-HCl pH 7.4, 75 mM KCl, 1.5 mM MgCl2 and protease/RNase inhibitors; 10 min at 37 C. Sucrose-cushion centrifugation and immunoblotting distinguish free and bound eIF6.
Family mapping
Q9Y3A5/SBDS_HUMAN is 250 aa, sequence version 4, matching current NP_057122.2 and InterPro exactly. PF01172 spans 14-100; the paper's structural domain I spans 2-96. The paper cites unversioned NP_057122. The intact protein includes other domains, and complete experimental tags/scars remain unresolved.
Limitations
SBDS alone is not sufficient. GDP/no nucleotide fail and GDPNP gives marginal release; these do not quantify a complete kinetic cycle. Ebp1/Nmd3 retention constrains specificity. The supplement was inaccessible, so full construct details and raw blots are unverified. Neither isolated SBDS_N activity nor direct 80S assembly is measured.

SBDS and EFL1 release truncated eIF6 from rabbit 60S particles

MECHANISTIC

Scope
Human SBDS/EFL1 and recombinant human eIF6(1-225) on salt-washed rabbit reticulocyte 60S subunits in Figure 3D. This mature-particle preparation is separate from mouse pre-60S.
Context
Preload 5 pmol rabbit 60S with excess eIF6(1-225) for 20 min on ice, remove free factor with a 100-kDa filter, and perform the 100-ul release assay with 5 pmol each SBDS/EFL1 and 100 uM GTP for 10 min at 37 C in the pH-7.4 release buffer. Compare bound/free immunoblots after sucrose pelleting.
Family mapping
Human whole SBDS maps to Q9Y3A5 and PF01172 14-100; the rabbit particle and truncated eIF6 are separate components. The SBDS and EFL1 tag states cannot be reconstructed from the accessible main Methods alone.
Limitations
Rpl28 retention and EDTA release controls are retained. The eIF6 truncation excludes S235, but this does not exclude every phosphorylation mechanism in cells. Mature rabbit particles are not treated as equivalent to mouse precursors or a wholly human system.

SBDS preparations alter 60S-dependent EFL1 GTP hydrolysis

MECHANISTIC

Scope
WT, R126T and K151N human SBDS preparations in the separate rabbit-60S phosphate-release assay of Figure 6B. R126T and K151N are outside PF01172.
Context
50 ul containing 5 nmol gamma-32P-GTP, 5 pmol rabbit 60S, 6.3 pmol SBDS when present and 40 pmol EFL1; 50 mM Tris pH 7.4, 2 mM MgCl2, 150 mM NaCl and 2 mM DTT; 30 min at 37 C. Charcoal separation and scintillation quantify Pi; bars report three experiments with SD.
Family mapping
Q9Y3A5 has R126 and K151 outside its PF01172 14-100 match, within the full multidomain SBDS molecule. These are whole-protein context effects, not mapped N-terminal catalytic residues.
Limitations
SBDS alone without 60S does not significantly activate EFL1. H96A EFL1 is a catalytic control. Hydrolysis and eIF6 release use different buffers and particles in separate vessels. K151N's nonsignificant difference from WT does not establish kinetic equivalence; no universal rate or stoichiometry is inferred.
