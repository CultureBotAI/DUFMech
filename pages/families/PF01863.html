Source description

This is a conserved domain containing the catalytic zinc-metallopeptidase (HExxH) catalytic motif. Proteins containing this domain are found in some archaebacteria, as well as Helicobacter pylori. The proteins are 190-240 amino acids long, with the C terminus being the most conserved region, containing three conserved histidines [InterPro reference PUB00098014,InterPro reference PUB00098016,InterPro reference PUB00098015,InterPro reference PUB00084997]. This domain is found in YgjP from E. coli [InterPro reference PUB00084997], a predicted metal-dependent hydrolase that hydrolyses UTP to UMP and diphosphate in vitro; and in MJ0123 from Methanocaldococcus jannaschii (also referred to as projannalysin) [InterPro reference PUB00098015].

Proabylysin intramolecular tail may suppress proteolysis

HYPOTHETICAL

Scope
Structural interpretation of recombinant proabylysin 4JIU and Figure 3A, not demonstrated protease activation.
Context
A Q101-V105 C-terminal segment occupies the putative substrate cleft in reverse substrate orientation, with V105 carboxylate coordinating Zn. Negative tested-substrate panels and unsuccessful activation trials are retained as limits.
Family mapping
Q9V1Y2/CAB49216.1, native numbering; deposited L2V scaffold, paper K2V discrepancy retained. PF01863 2-88 contains H60/E61/H64 and H72, but not Q101-V105. Whole-protein architecture, not a domain-only mechanism.
Limitations
Structural occupancy supports a proposed explanation for inactivity, not proof of reversible inhibition or a functional zymogen. No active abylysin was obtained. Tail truncation destabilizes the protein, and an inactive-ancestor interpretation remains possible. No family-wide protease claim.

Projannalysin partner helix may suppress proteolysis

HYPOTHETICAL

Scope
Structural interpretation of the recombinant projannalysin dimer 4JIX and Figure 3B-D, kept distinct from the proabylysin intramolecular model.
Context
Partner E102-I110 helix lies across each putative cleft. I110 carboxylate is 4.1-4.2 A from Zn, unlike proabylysin V105 coordination. Purified protein is dimeric by size exclusion; crystallographic geometry alone does not prove intracellular dimerization or catalytic activity.
Family mapping
MJ0123 Q57587 version 1 equals AAB98111.1, 110 aa; JAL-1/DSM 2661 taxon 243232. PF01863 7-95 contains H69/E70/H73 and H81 but excludes E102-I110. PDB 4JIX has K2A plus residual N-terminal GP after His6/TEV cleavage; native numbering, not polymer seq_id, is used.
Limitations
The proposed trans-blocking mechanism is not directly tested by a folded active product. The five-residue deletion and limited-proteolysis attempts were unsuccessful. Negative assays do not establish inactivity against every possible substrate; the inactive-ancestor alternative remains open.

Seed status
A name-based classification of frozen InterPro/Pfam metadata. UNKNOWN CANDIDATE means the metadata explicitly says the function is unknown. KNOWN HISTORICAL DUF means a DUF name matched without that wording; this heuristic is not proof of a known function and does not establish experimental characterization. EX DUF means Pfam renamed the family away from a DUF/UPF name, as recorded in Pfam's previous identifiers; a rename usually follows published work on some members but is naming history, not proof of function for every member. Some EX DUF families' current names or descriptions still say the function is unknown; their classification basis lists this, and scoring does not treat them as characterized. FALSE POSITIVE TEXT HIT means the naming rules did not match.
Characterization
A separate evidence-scoring result. UNSCORED means no score has been calculated; it is not evidence that the family lacks a known function. Missing scores are not negative evidence. PARTIALLY CHARACTERIZED has partial support; KNOWN HISTORICAL DUF can inherit the seed label or reflect a known-function signal. Neither label implies experimental validation.
Evidence counts
Source-specific signals, not unique proteins, publications, or confidence scores. Known counts Rhea reactions and experimentally supported GO molecular-function annotations. Partial counts functional/domain assignments. Context counts structural, environmental, orthology, interaction and neighborhood signals; context alone does not assign function. Repeated annotations may not be independent.
