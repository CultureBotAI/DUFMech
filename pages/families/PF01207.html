PF01207: Dihydrouridine synthase (Dus)
IPR035587: integrated entry for Dihydrouridine synthase (Dus)

Source description

Members of this family catalyse the reduction of the 5,6-double bond of a uridine residue on tRNA. Dihydrouridine modification of tRNA is widely observed in prokaryotes and eukaryotes, and also in some archae. Most dihydrouridines are found in the D loop of t-RNAs. The role of dihydrouridine in tRNA is currently unknown, but may increase conformational flexibility of the tRNA. It is likely that different family members have different substrate specificities, which may overlap. Dus 1 (Q9HGN6 (UniProtKB protein)) from Saccharomyces cerevisiae acts on pre-tRNA-Phe, while Dus 2 (P53720 (UniProtKB protein)) acts on pre-tRNA-Tyr and pre-tRNA-Leu. Dus 1 is active as a single subunit, requiring NADPH or NADH, and is stimulated by the presence of FAD [InterPro reference PUB00014304]. Some family members may be targeted to the mitochondria and even have a role in mitochondria [InterPro reference PUB00014304].

NADPH reduces the flavin of purified yeast Dus2

MECHANISTIC

Scope
Recombinant WT Saccharomyces cerevisiae Dus2 in Rider 2009 Figure 2 and reductive-half-reaction Results. This is an anaerobic purified-protein assay, not cellular RNA modification.
Context
Mix 15 uM oxidized enzyme with anaerobic NADPH concentration series at 4 C in 100 mM HEPES pH 7.5, 100 mM NaCl, 10 mM MgCl2 and 10% glycerol. Stopped-flow absorbance at 450 nm measures flavin reduction. DTT is removed immediately before assays.
Family mapping
P53720/DUS2_YEAST is SMM1/DUS2/YNR015W, 384 aa, sequence version 1, with PF01207 at 32-352. UniProt and InterPro sequences agree. The study uses the supplied pEfx-4b yeast DUS2 plasmid and BL21(DE3) expression, cobalt-affinity and DEAE purification. Exact historical tag junctions and clone sequence remain unverified.
Limitations
Minor slower spectral phases may reflect damaged protein. The observed reduction does not independently establish the proposed NADP dissociation rate or every hydride-transfer microstep. FMN in this preparation is not a claim that all family members have identical cofactor or substrate behavior.

Prereduced Dus2 forms dihydrouridine in a synthetic tRNA preparation

MECHANISTIC

Scope
WT yeast Dus2 acting on the reported synthetic pre-tRNA-Leu preparation in Figure 3 and oxidative-half-reaction Results. No nucleotide position is independently assigned by this endpoint.
Context
Dithionite-prereduced enzyme reacts anaerobically at 25 C in the pH-7.5 HEPES/NaCl/MgCl2/glycerol buffer. Results give 60 uM enzyme and 700 uM RNA, whereas the caption gives 35 uM enzyme. Flavin reoxidation is followed over hours; a separate alkaline-ring-opening A550 colorimetric assay measures dihydrouridine against a standard curve.
Family mapping
Intact yeast Dus2 maps to P53720 and PF01207 32-352. The published pre/intronless template strings differ by a promoter-containing prefix rather than an evident intron, so the exact assayed RNA sequence is not reconstructed from those strings.
Limitations
The enzyme concentration and 450/457-nm reporting differ between caption and Results. Product colorimetry supports dihydrouridine formation but is not site-resolved sequencing or mass spectrometry. Exact RNA sequence, matched no-enzyme raw data and stoichiometry were not independently verified; no precise kinetic constant or C117 proton-transfer edge is adopted.

Aerobic yeast Dus2 treatment lowers dihydrouridine in bulk bacterial tRNA

MECHANISTIC

Scope
Recombinant yeast Dus2 with bulk E. coli tRNA in the 18-h reverse-reaction experiment. This is a mixed-species in-vitro endpoint.
Context
Treat 90 uM bulk E. coli tRNA with 40 uM Dus2 aerobically for 18 h and assay dihydrouridine content. General kinetics default to 4 C, but the endpoint paragraph does not explicitly restate temperature. Do not borrow the anaerobic stopped-flow conditions as an exact endpoint recipe.
Family mapping
The enzyme maps to P53720 with PF01207 32-352; the bacterial RNA mixture is a separate substrate whose strain and individual tRNA composition are not resolved. It is not a pure yeast physiological system.
Limitations
The measured content decreases but is not eliminated. The assay does not identify each affected tRNA or nucleotide, measure cellular reverse flux, or independently characterize every oxidation product. Oxygen-supported endpoint turnover is distinct from the short anaerobic spectral assay.
