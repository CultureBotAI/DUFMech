PF00668: Condensation domain
IPR001242: integrated entry for Condensation domain

Source description

This domain is found in many multi-domain enzymes which synthesise peptide antibiotics. This domain catalyses a condensation reaction to form peptide bonds in non- ribosomal peptide biosynthesis. It is usually found to the carboxy side of a phosphopantetheine binding domain (PF00550 (Pfam entry)). It has been shown that mutations in the HHXXXDG motif abolish activity suggesting this is part of the active site [InterPro reference PUB00007135].

Causal graphs

Joint TycB1 and GrsA preparations produce a cyclic dipeptide

MECHANISTIC

Scope
Reconstituted recombinant holo-TycB1 Pro-CAT first module and GrsA Phe-ATE, with separately tested TycB1 R62A, H147R and H147V substitutions. This is neither full tyrocidine biosynthesis nor an isolated condensation-domain sufficiency assay.
Context
C-terminal His6-tagged TycB1 variants were produced with gsp phosphopantetheinyl-transferase expression and nickel-affinity purification. The DKP assay separately preincubates each enzyme at 1 uM with 2 mM ATP and its amino-acid substrate for 3 min at 37 C, mixes equal volumes, and incubates 45 min at 37 C before extraction and HPLC-MS. Assay buffer contains 50 mM HEPES pH 8.0, 100 mM NaCl, 10 mM MgCl2 and 1 mM EDTA. The HTML gives 0.5 M phenylalanine and 0.5 mM proline before mixing; the phenylalanine unit remains unresolved rather than being silently corrected. Figure 4 uses three consecutive experiments from the same protein batch and thiolation normalization.
Family mapping
TycB is UniProtKB:O30408, NCBIProtein:AAC45929.1, nucleotide AF004835, 3587 residues, sequence version 1. Current reference taxon is Brevibacillus parabrevis 54914; the sequence-linked primary operon study names historical Bacillus brevis ATCC 8185. Exact UniProt/InterPro sequence agreement places the first PF00668 interval at 9-454, containing R62 and H147. The other three PF00668 matches, including the terminal epimerization region, are not assigned this reaction. Complete construct boundaries and cloning junctions were not independently reconstructed. The paper's GrsA partner is named but not assigned an unverified accession from the two current reviewed GrsA references.
Limitations
DKP is an observed product of the joint coupled system; it is an indirect readout of peptide condensation followed by release/cyclization. No elementary proton transfer, isolated-domain reaction or family-wide substrate specificity is established. Retained adenylation and thiolation do not prove unchanged local C-domain folding. Absolute detection limits and independent-purification replication are not reported here. Source D151N/D151A, Y166F/Y151F and Figure 1 tycB/tycA inconsistencies remain open; no ambiguous D151 edge is adopted. Figure captions and text were inspected, not the unavailable plotted images or raw chromatograms.
