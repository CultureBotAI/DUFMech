Source description

This family is expressed by almost all bacterial and eukaryotic genomes but not by archaea. Its function is to down-regulate protein synthesis under conditions of nutrient shortage, and it does this by binding to protein L14 of the large ribosomal subunit, thus acting as a ribosomal silencing factor (RsfS) by blocking the joining of the ribosomal subunits [InterPro reference PUB00064845]. This family is structurally homologous to nucleotidyltransferases.

Causal graphs

E. coli RsfS suppresses ribosome association and oligo(Phe) synthesis

MECHANISTIC

Scope
Purified N-terminal His6-tagged E. coli b0637 RsfS, called RsfA in the paper, expressed in BL21(DE3); E. coli ribosomal subunits and purified translation factors.
Context
Preincubate 18 pmol 50S with 180 ug poly(U) and with or without 360 pmol RsfS in 90 uL for 10 min at 37 C, then 10 pmol 30S for 10 min. Buffer contains 20 mM HEPES pH 7.6 at 0 C, 4.5 mM Mg acetate, 150 mM K acetate, 4 mM beta-mercaptoethanol, 2 mM spermidine and 0.05 mM spermine. Sister aliquots undergo sucrose gradients or precharged Phe-tRNA/factor-dependent synthesis for 2 min at 30 C.
Family mapping
b0637 maps to UniProtKB:P0AAT6, sequence version 1, 105 residues; PF02410 spans 9-104. The experiment uses the tagged protein, not an isolated domain interval.
Limitations
The two endpoints are measured separately in sister aliquots, not independent replication. Preassembled programmed 70S does not show the same strong suppression; no literally zero effect is claimed. Steric contacts from docking and binding assays are not separate observed causal steps. Human mitochondrial and later pre-50S biogenesis contexts remain separate.

RsfS deletion prolongs growth adaptation after an LB-to-M9 shift

PHENOTYPIC

Scope
Whole-gene b0637 deletion in E. coli BW25113 derivatives versus matched wild type; separate cellular phenotype from the purified-subunit experiment.
Context
Overnight LB cultures diluted to OD600 0.005 in M9, 37 C and 200 rpm; OD600 monitored up to 40 h. Complementation uses cassette-excised mutants and tac-driven C-terminal His-tagged RsfS induced with 0.2 mM IPTG about 3 h after transfer.
Family mapping
The paper identifies the deleted locus as b0637, corresponding to P0AAT6 and PF02410 at reference residues 9-104. This is not a domain-specific perturbation or independent resequencing of BW25113.
Limitations
The phenotype is a prolonged growth plateau after initial growth, not immediate death or a measured energy deficit. Native-promoter complementation failed under the tested conditions, while induced tac expression rescued; the two are not conflated. No direct metabolic chain from the in-vitro assembly effect is asserted.

Seed status
A name-based classification of frozen InterPro/Pfam metadata. UNKNOWN CANDIDATE means the metadata explicitly says the function is unknown. KNOWN HISTORICAL DUF means a DUF name matched without that wording; this heuristic is not proof of a known function and does not establish experimental characterization. EX DUF means Pfam renamed the family away from a DUF/UPF name, as recorded in Pfam's previous identifiers; a rename usually follows published work on some members but is naming history, not proof of function for every member. Some EX DUF families' current names or descriptions still say the function is unknown; their classification basis lists this, and scoring does not treat them as characterized. FALSE POSITIVE TEXT HIT means the naming rules did not match.
Characterization
A separate evidence-scoring result. UNSCORED means no score has been calculated; it is not evidence that the family lacks a known function. Missing scores are not negative evidence. PARTIALLY CHARACTERIZED has partial support; KNOWN HISTORICAL DUF can inherit the seed label or reflect a known-function signal. Neither label implies experimental validation.

Partial counts functional/domain assignments. Context counts structural, environmental, orthology, interaction and neighborhood signals; context alone does not assign function. Repeated annotations may not be independent.
