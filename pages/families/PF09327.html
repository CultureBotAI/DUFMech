Source description

The Tail Tip Fiber Protein family includes proteins involved in the attachment and penetration of bacteriophages into host cells. Members of this family are responsible for recognizing and irreversibly binding to specific host receptors, which triggers structural changes in the viral tail necessary for the injection of viral DNA into the host. This process is critical for the successful infection and propagation of the virus. The family includes proteins that initiate distal tail tip assembly during phage tail assembly and play a key role in the virus entry by mediating the attachment to the host receptor and inducing DNA ejection through the receptor's pore. This entry represents the beta prism domain which is formed from a homotrimeric complex of members of this family.

Seed status

EX DUF means Pfam renamed the family away from a DUF/UPF name, as recorded in Pfam's previous identifiers; a rename usually follows published work on some members but is naming history, not proof of function for every member. Some EX DUF families' current names or descriptions still say the function is unknown; their classification basis lists this, and scoring does not treat them as characterized. FALSE POSITIVE TEXT HIT means the naming rules did not match.
